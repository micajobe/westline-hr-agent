# Performance & Compensation Review Process

## 1. Scope, and what this document deliberately does not do

This document describes the **process and the calendar** by which Westline staff performance and
compensation are reviewed. It applies to staff only; contractor and creator partner work is reviewed
under their own agreements, and for creator partners the tier review in `CREATOR` §1.3 is the
equivalent mechanism. See `HANDBOOK` §2.

**This document does not describe outcomes.** It does not state what any individual will be paid, what
any individual's rating was or will be, whether any individual will receive an increase, the size of
any increase, or the size of the merit pool in any given year. Those are individual and confidential,
they are not policy, and no part of this document can be read to predict them.

If your question is "will I get a raise this cycle", or "what rating will I get", this document cannot
answer it and neither can anyone reading it on your behalf. That conversation is between you and your
newsroom lead, and the right time to have it is the mid-year check-in in §3.2. Asking your lead
directly is the correct move, not a faux pas.

## 2. Principles

Reviews are about work, not personality. Expectations are set at the start of a cycle rather than
revealed at the end. Nothing in a review should be new information: feedback is continuous and the
review documents it. Ratings are moderated across a market so that a lead's personal scale does not
decide someone's year.

## 3. The annual cycle

| Stage | Timing | Who |
|---|---|---|
| Objectives set for the year | January | Staff member and newsroom lead |
| Mid-year check-in | June | Staff member and newsroom lead |
| Self-assessment | Late September | Staff member |
| Lead assessment | Early October | Newsroom lead |
| Calibration | Late October | Market director, leads, People & Culture |
| Review conversation | November | Staff member and newsroom lead |
| Compensation decisions communicated | Late January | Newsroom lead |
| Changes effective | March 1 | Payroll |

### 3.1 Objectives

Three to five objectives, written jointly, covering output, craft and contribution to the team. They
may be revised mid-year by agreement, and a revision is recorded.

### 3.2 The mid-year check-in

A documented conversation in June covering progress, obstacles, and whether the objectives still make
sense. It is not rated. It is the point in the cycle where a concern must be raised if it is going to
count in the review, and it is the natural place to ask where you stand.

### 3.3 Self-assessment and lead assessment

The self-assessment is written first and the lead reads it before writing theirs. Both are shared in
full with the staff member before the review conversation, so nobody hears a judgement for the first
time in the room.

### 3.4 Calibration

Leads present their assessments to the market director and a People & Culture partner and adjust for
consistency. Calibration is not a forced distribution: there is no quota of high or low ratings and no
requirement that any rating be assigned to anyone.

### 3.5 The review conversation

Covers the assessment, the rating, development, and the objectives for the next cycle. It does **not**
cover compensation, which is communicated separately in late January under §4.

## 4. Compensation review

### 4.1 What is reviewed

Base pay against the market band for the role and the home market, the performance rating, and any
change in scope during the year.

### 4.2 Timing

Compensation decisions are made in December and January and communicated in **late January**, with
changes effective **March 1**. The two-month gap between the review conversation and the compensation
conversation is deliberate: it keeps the development conversation in November from becoming a
negotiation.

### 4.3 What determines an outcome

The merit pool is set annually by the executive against Westline's financial performance, and the
distribution within a market is decided at calibration. Neither the pool nor any individual outcome is
published, and neither is described anywhere in this document.

### 4.4 Off-cycle changes

Off-cycle increases are made for a promotion, a substantial and lasting change in scope, or a
market-band correction. They are proposed by the market director and approved by People & Culture.
There is no application route for an off-cycle increase; raise it with your lead.

## 5. Special cases

### 5.1 New staff

Staff who joined after July 1 have a shortened first cycle with objectives and a review conversation
but no rating, and they are included in the following year's compensation review.

### 5.2 Staff on leave

A staff member on leave for more than 8 weeks of the cycle is assessed on the portion actually worked.
The absence itself is never a performance factor, and a parental, medical or bereavement leave does not
reduce a rating. `LEAVE` §6.5.

### 5.3 Staff who changed role

Assessed against the objectives in force during each part of the year, by each lead, with the market
director reconciling.

## 6. Disagreement

### 6.1 Adding a statement

A staff member who disagrees with an assessment may add a written statement to the record. It is filed
with the review and read at the next calibration.

### 6.2 Review of process

A staff member may ask a People & Culture partner to review whether the **process** was followed —
whether objectives were set, whether the mid-year check-in happened, whether calibration was applied
consistently. People & Culture does not re-rate the work; it checks the process and requires a lead to
redo a step that was skipped.

### 6.3 When it is a conduct matter

Where a review is alleged to be retaliation, discrimination or harassment, it stops being a
performance question and is handled under `CONDUCT` §5, including the direct route to a People &
Culture partner in `CONDUCT` §5.1.

## 7. Performance concerns

Serious or sustained concerns are managed through a documented improvement plan with specific,
measurable expectations and a defined period, normally 60 to 90 days, with fortnightly check-ins and a
People & Culture partner involved throughout. An improvement plan is not a formality preceding a
dismissal; staff complete them and continue.

## 8. The rating scale

Ratings describe the year's work against the objectives set in §3.1. They do not describe a person,
they are not a ranking against colleagues, and they do not by themselves determine a compensation
outcome — §4.3 says what does, and says that the outcome is not published anywhere.

**Exceptional.** Delivered the objectives and materially raised the standard of the team's work.
Evidenced by specific work, not by effort or by hours.

**Strong.** Delivered the objectives consistently, including through the year's harder stretches. This
is the rating most people receive most years, and it is a good year.

**Effective.** Delivered most of the objectives, with gaps that were named at the mid-year check-in
and are understood.

**Developing.** Did not deliver against some objectives. Appropriate for someone new to a role, and it
carries a development plan rather than a consequence.

**Below expectations.** Sustained shortfall against objectives that were clear and achievable. Never a
first notification — if this rating is a surprise, §2 was not followed and §6.2 is the route.

There is no quota for any rating (§3.4). A market in which everyone did well can rate everyone
Strong, and calibration will not manufacture a spread.

## 9. Questions we are asked often

**Will I get a raise this cycle?** This document cannot tell you. It describes the process and the
calendar and explicitly does not describe outcomes (§1). Neither the merit pool nor any individual
outcome is published (§4.3). Ask your newsroom lead — the mid-year check-in in §3.2 is the designed
moment for it, and asking directly is the correct move.

**When will I find out?** Ratings at the review conversation in November (§3.5). Compensation
decisions in late January, effective March 1 (§4.2). The gap is deliberate: it keeps November's
development conversation from becoming a negotiation.

**Can I see what my lead wrote?** Yes. Both assessments are shared in full before the review
conversation, so nothing is heard for the first time in the room (§3.3).

**I disagree with my rating.** Add a written statement to the record, which is read at the next
calibration (§6.1). If the issue is that the process was not followed — no objectives set, no mid-year
check-in — ask a People & Culture partner to review it (§6.2). People & Culture does not re-rate the
work; it requires a skipped step to be redone.

**I think my review was retaliation for a complaint.** That stops being a performance question. It is
handled under `CONDUCT` §5, including the direct route to a People & Culture partner in `CONDUCT`
§5.1, and retaliation is a breach in its own right under `CONDUCT` §7.2.

**I was on leave for four months.** You are assessed on the portion of the cycle you worked, and the
absence is never a performance factor (§5.2, `LEAVE` §6.5).

**I joined in September.** Shortened first cycle: objectives and a review conversation, no rating, and
you are in the following year's compensation review (§5.1).

**I moved roles mid-year.** Assessed against the objectives in force for each part of the year, by
each lead, with the market director reconciling (§5.3).

**I'm a contractor / creator partner. Where is my review?** This policy does not apply to you
(`HANDBOOK` §2). Contractor work is reviewed under the engagement. For creator partners the equivalent
is the six-monthly tier review in `CREATOR` §1.3.

**Is an improvement plan the start of a dismissal?** No. It is a documented plan with measurable
expectations over 60 to 90 days, with fortnightly check-ins and a People & Culture partner involved
throughout (§7). Staff complete them and continue.

## 10. Records

Objectives, self-assessments, lead assessments, ratings, any statement added under §6.1, and
improvement plans are held by People & Culture in the personnel record for the statutory retention
period of the province of employment (`INFOSEC` §8.2). They are visible to the staff member, to their
current lead, to the market director, and to People & Culture — and to nobody else. A new lead
inheriting a team sees the prior year's record; they do not see records from before that.

Calibration discussions are not minuted at the individual level. What is recorded is the rating that
resulted, not what was said about a person in the room.

Compensation decisions are held by People & Culture and Finance. Leads are told the outcome for their
own reports so they can deliver it, and are not told anyone else's.
