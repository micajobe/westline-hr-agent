# Social Media & Personal Brand

This document binds staff, contractors and creator partners. The obligations differ by class and each
section says which. See `HANDBOOK` §2.

## 1. The principle

Your audience does not sort your posts by which contract they were made under. Anything you publish
that is connected to Westline — by your bio, by your byline, by the work itself — is read as Westline
speaking. That is why a policy about personal accounts exists at all.

## 2. Westline accounts

### 2.1 Who may post

Only people authorised by the market's social editor post from a Westline handle. Access is granted
through `ONBOARD` §4 and revoked the same day an engagement ends under `ONBOARD` §6.

### 2.2 Voice and errors

Westline handles carry the same standards as broadcast: verified before posted, attributed, corrected
under §6. A deleted post is not a correction. Where a post must be removed, the correction is posted
separately and explains what was removed.

### 2.3 Credentials

Westline account credentials are never shared, never stored outside the approved password manager, and
never used on an unmanaged device. `INFOSEC` §2 governs.

## 3. Personal accounts — staff

### 3.1 You are identifiable

If your bio names Westline, or you are recognisable from on-air work, your personal account is a
public-facing channel and `CONDUCT` §4.1 applies to it.

### 3.2 Breaking news

Do not break Westline stories on a personal account before they are published by Westline. Do not post
newsroom-sourced material that has not been published. Do not post from inside an embargo.

### 3.3 Political expression

Staff whose work touches politics, public policy or elections — reporters, producers, hosts, editors
on those files — do not publicly endorse candidates or parties, do not donate publicly, do not
display partisan material, and do not sign public petitions on contested political questions. This is
narrow by design: it covers people whose impartiality on those subjects is the product.

Staff outside those roles may participate in civic life. Everyone retains the right to speak about
their own working conditions, which is protected and is not a breach of this section.

### 3.4 Commenting on Westline

Public criticism of Westline's editorial decisions belongs inside the newsroom first, through
`EDITORIAL` §7.4. Nothing in this section prevents you from raising a concern with a regulator, from
speaking about your working conditions, or from making a protected disclosure.

## 4. Personal accounts — contractors and creator partners

### 4.1 Contractors

While engaged, do not publish material gathered on a Westline assignment before Westline publishes it,
and do not present yourself as speaking for Westline. Otherwise your accounts are your own.

### 4.2 Creator partners

Your channel is the reason we work with you, and this policy does not try to run it. Two things bind
you:

- **Disclosure.** Sponsored work is disclosed to the `EDITORIAL` §4 standard, including sponsored work
  for brands that have nothing to do with Westline. `CREATOR` §6.2 states this obligation and it is
  deliberately stricter than most platform rules.
- **Notice.** Outside brand deals carry the 10-day written notice in `CREATOR` §6.2, so that we can
  check a live exclusivity window under `CREATOR` §6.1.

Beyond those, publish what you like. Westline does not approve your non-Westline content.

## 5. Sponsored and affiliate content

Every material connection is disclosed under `EDITORIAL` §4, in the platform's native disclosure tool
**and** in the caption per `EDITORIAL` §4.3. The platform tool alone is not sufficient, because it
renders inconsistently across surfaces.

Affiliate links are disclosed in the same post, adjacent to the link, not in a bio or a pinned comment.

Staff do not run affiliate arrangements on subjects they cover — that is a `EDITORIAL` §6 conflict —
and take outside paid media work only with the approvals in `EDITORIAL` §6.3.

## 6. Corrections on social

Within **24 hours** of confirming an error: correct on the same channel, in the same format, at the
same prominence. For a post that cannot be edited, post a correction and link it from the original.
Substantive errors are logged under `EDITORIAL` §5.3.

## 7. Harassment and account security

### 7.1 Do not engage

Do not argue with harassers and do not post screenshots of a harasser's account, which escalates.
Block, report to the platform, and tell your lead and the Editorial Standards desk.

### 7.2 What Westline does

Westline will escalate to platform trust-and-safety contacts, support a police report where warranted,
arrange a temporary byline or handle change, and reassign a story where continuing to cover it is the
cause of the harassment. `CONDUCT` §4.3 is the policy basis. Creator partners route through Creator
Partnerships and receive the same support.

### 7.3 Account security

Multi-factor authentication is required on every account used for Westline work, personal accounts
included where your work depends on them. Doxxing risk is reduced by keeping home location, vehicle
and family details off public accounts. `INFOSEC` §2.3 covers authentication.

## 8. Enforcement

Breaches are handled under `CONDUCT` §8 for staff and contractors and under `CREATOR` §8.2 for creator
partners. An undisclosed sponsorship is treated as a serious breach in every class, because it is the
one failure the audience cannot detect and cannot forgive.

## 9. Platform-specific notes

The obligations in §5 and §6 are platform-independent. These notes cover where they are most often got
wrong.

### 9.1 Short-form vertical video

The caption tag in `EDITORIAL` §4.3 must sit in the first two lines *as rendered on that platform*,
which is usually shorter than the composer suggests. Check it in the published preview, not in the
draft. The on-screen label has to be re-laid-out for the vertical safe area — `EDITORIAL` §8.2 covers
the failure in detail, and it is the most common disclosure defect the Editorial Standards desk finds.

### 9.2 Stories and ephemeral posts

Ephemeral does not mean undisclosed. A story carrying sponsored content carries the label and the tag
for every frame it appears in, because a viewer may see only one. A correction to a story is published
as a new story within 24 hours **and** as a permanent post, since the original will expire.

### 9.3 Live

Sponsorship is disclosed verbally at the start of a live and again before any read, per
`EDITORIAL` §4.4. A live that runs long carries a repeat verbal disclosure at least every 15 minutes.
Comments during a live are moderated by someone other than the host.

### 9.4 Text-first platforms and threads

The disclosure goes in the first post of a thread, not in a reply, because replies are read out of
order and quoted individually.

### 9.5 Direct messages

A source or a member of the public reaching you by direct message is contacting Westline. Sensitive
contact moves to the approved encrypted messenger under `INFOSEC` §3.3, and a confidential source's
identity never sits in a platform inbox.

## 10. Worked examples

### 10.1 A staff reporter posts about a story before it airs

Breach of §3.2. Newsroom-sourced material is not published on a personal account before Westline
publishes it, and an embargo binds the individual as much as the outlet.

### 10.2 A politics producer signs a public petition on a contested policy

Breach of §3.3 for someone whose file is politics or public policy. The same act by a staff member
in, say, finance is not a breach — §3.3 is deliberately narrow and covers people whose impartiality on
those subjects is the product.

### 10.3 A host is being harassed after a story

Do not engage and do not post screenshots of the harasser (§7.1). Report to the platform, tell the
lead and the Editorial Standards desk. Westline escalates to platform trust-and-safety, supports a
police report where warranted, and will reassign the story where covering it is the cause (§7.2,
`CONDUCT` §4.3). A creator partner in the same position routes through Creator Partnerships and gets
the same support.

### 10.4 A creator partner takes a brand deal Westline has nothing to do with

Permitted, with two obligations: ten days' written notice to Creator Partnerships naming the brand and
category (`CREATOR` §6.2), and disclosure on their own channel to the `EDITORIAL` §4 standard (§4.2
above). Westline does not approve the content and does not get a say in who they work with; it can
only tell them whether the category collides with a live exclusivity window.

### 10.5 A Westline account posts an error

Correct, do not delete (§2.2). Where the post cannot be edited, publish a correction and link it from
the original within 24 hours (§6). A silent deletion is not a correction and, for a substantive error,
is a second failure on top of the first.

## 11. Bios, handles and identity

### 11.1 Naming Westline in a bio

Staff may name Westline in a personal bio and most do. Doing so brings the account inside §3.1 and
`CONDUCT` §4.1, which is the trade. A bio that names Westline carries the standard disclaimer — that
views are the individual's — but a disclaimer does not release you from either section, and no
audience has ever read one as doing so.

Contractors describe themselves as working with Westline, not for Westline, because the second is
inaccurate and creates an impression of editorial authority they do not have. Creator partners
describe themselves as a Westline creator partner, which is what the agreement says.

### 11.2 Handles that include the brand

Personal handles do not incorporate "Westline", a market brand, or a show name. Those are Westline
marks and a handle carrying one is indistinguishable from an official account. Existing handles
predating this version are reviewed case by case by the Editorial Standards desk; new ones are not
approved.

### 11.3 Leaving Westline

Remove Westline from bios within five business days of the last working day. Access to Westline
handles is revoked at end of day on the last working day under `ONBOARD` §6.1, and the follower count
on a Westline handle is Westline's, not the departing person's. Personal accounts, including ones
grown while at Westline, are the individual's and go with them.

## 12. Advertising, endorsement and the boundary with editorial

`EDITORIAL` §3.2 keeps sponsors out of editorial. This section is the social-facing half of the same
rule, and it is where the boundary is most often blurred by accident.

Do not post promotional content for a Westline advertiser on a personal account as a favour, a
courtesy, or because someone in sales asked. If it is paid, it is sponsored content and every
requirement in §5 applies. If it is unpaid, it is an endorsement of an advertiser by someone the
audience associates with Westline editorial, which is the exact impression `EDITORIAL` §3.2 exists to
prevent.

Do not amplify a sponsor's own posts from an account that also carries your byline, beyond sharing
Westline's own published sponsored segment with its disclosure intact.

Where a sponsor tags you and asks for a repost, the answer is the licence question in `CREATOR` §4.5
for creator partners, and for staff it is a referral to the market's social editor. Neither of those
is a decision to make in the moment on a phone.
