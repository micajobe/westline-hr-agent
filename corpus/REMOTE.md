# Remote, Hybrid & Multi-Market Work

This policy applies to Westline staff. Contractors and creator partners choose their own place of
work under their own agreements; they remain bound by `INFOSEC` and `SAFETY` wherever they work.
See `HANDBOOK` §2.

## 1. The home market default

### 1.1 Your home market

Every staff member has a home market recorded in the people directory: Calgary, Edmonton,
Vancouver, Kelowna, Saskatoon or Winnipeg. Your home market determines your statutory holidays
(`PTO` §5.1), your per diem rate (`EXPENSE` §3.2), your payroll province, and which office you are
expected in.

Your home market is set when you are hired and changes only by agreement with People & Culture. It
is not changed by working somewhere else temporarily, however long the trip. A change of home
market is a change to your terms of employment and triggers a payroll province change, a benefits
review and, in most cases, a compensation review against the new market's band.

### 1.2 In-office expectation

Staff attached to a market with a Westline office are expected in that office **three days per
week**, including any day they are on a studio call sheet or a live show rundown. Newsroom leads
set which three days for their team and publish them a month ahead.

Staff in roles designated fully remote in their employment agreement — currently parts of digital,
audience and finance — have no in-office expectation and are instead expected in their market
office for **four days per quarter**, normally aligned to planning weeks.

### 1.3 Ad-hoc days at home

Working from home on a day that is not one of your in-office days needs no approval and no
notification beyond your team's normal availability practice. Nothing in this policy requires you
to ask permission to work from your own home in your own market.

## 2. Working from another Westline market

### 2.1 Notification

Working from another Westline market — Calgary staff working a week out of the Vancouver office,
for example — requires that you **notify your newsroom lead at least 5 business days in advance**
and that a desk is booked through the market's office coordinator. It does not require approval,
and it does not change your home market, your payroll province, your statutory holidays or your per
diem rate.

### 2.2 What is and is not an expense

Travel to another Westline market for your own convenience is not a business expense. Nothing is
reimbursable: not the flight, not the accommodation, not meals. Travel to another market at
Westline's request is a business trip and is reimbursable under `EXPENSE` §3.

The distinction is who asked. If your lead asked you to be there, it is a trip. If you asked to be
there, it is your trip.

### 2.3 Duration

There is no limit on time spent working from another Westline market inside your home province. A
stay of more than four consecutive weeks in a market in a **different province** is an
out-of-province arrangement and §3 applies, even though both locations are Westline offices.

## 3. Working temporarily outside your home province

### 3.1 Four weeks or less

A temporary arrangement of **four consecutive weeks or less** outside your home province, but
inside Canada, is self-approved with your newsroom lead's sign-off. Record it in the scheduling
system so that payroll can see it. No People & Culture review and no Finance review is required at
this length.

You remain subject to your home market's in-office expectation, which in practice means a lead
approving a four-week arrangement is also agreeing to waive in-office days for that period.

### 3.2 More than four weeks

An arrangement of **more than four consecutive weeks** outside your home province requires review
and written approval by **both People & Culture and Finance**, requested at least **6 weeks** in
advance. The review covers payroll withholding in the destination province, workers' compensation
coverage, and whether the length of stay creates a permanent-establishment or residency question
for Westline or for you. `EXPENSE` §8 describes the tax and payroll consequences in detail.

Approval is for a stated end date. Extending an approved arrangement requires a fresh review, not
an email.

### 3.3 Any work outside Canada

**Any** work outside Canada requires People & Culture and Finance approval before departure,
regardless of duration — including a single day. There is no self-approved tier for out-of-country
work. Request at least **6 weeks** in advance.

The reasons are cumulative and none of them is a formality:

- Payroll and tax treatment changes at the border, not at four weeks. `EXPENSE` §8.2 covers
  withholding obligations that can attach on day one in some jurisdictions.
- Westline's group benefits and disability coverage have out-of-country limits described in
  `BENEFITS` §5.3.
- Crossing a border with a device that can reach newsroom systems is a security event governed by
  `INFOSEC` §6, which has its own notification requirement and its own lead time.
- Where you will be doing journalism rather than desk work, `SAFETY` §2 applies and a risk
  assessment is required before travel.

### 3.4 Annual limit on out-of-country work

Approved out-of-country work is capped at **8 weeks in aggregate per calendar year** per staff
member. The cap exists because the payroll and residency exposure is cumulative, so two separate
five-week arrangements in one year are not two independent decisions. Requests that would exceed
the cap are declined; there is no market director override.

### 3.5 What approval does not cover

An approved out-of-province or out-of-country arrangement does not authorise you to:

- carry source material or unpublished investigative work across a border — see `INFOSEC` §6.3,
  which prohibits it outright rather than regulating it;
- represent Westline to authorities, broadcasters or regulators in the destination;
- accept local commissions or brand work while abroad — `SOCIAL` §5 and, for the conflict rules,
  `EDITORIAL` §6.

## 4. Approval summary

| Arrangement | Notice | Approver | Also required |
|---|---|---|---|
| Home, in your own market | None | None | — |
| Another Westline market, same province | 5 business days | Notify newsroom lead | Desk booking |
| Another Westline market, different province, ≤ 4 weeks | 5 business days | Newsroom lead sign-off | Record in scheduling system |
| Anywhere in Canada outside home province, ≤ 4 weeks | Before departure | Newsroom lead sign-off | Record in scheduling system |
| Anywhere in Canada outside home province, > 4 weeks | 6 weeks | People & Culture **and** Finance | Payroll review (`EXPENSE` §8) |
| Outside Canada, any duration | 6 weeks | People & Culture **and** Finance | `INFOSEC` §6 border notification; `BENEFITS` §5.3 coverage check; 8-week annual cap (§3.4) |

## 5. Equipment, connectivity and workspace

### 5.1 What Westline provides

Westline provides a laptop and the software entitlements for your role wherever you work. It does
not provide a second monitor, a desk, a chair or an internet connection as a matter of course; the
home-office allowance in `EXPENSE` §4 covers those within a cap.

### 5.2 Connectivity expectations

Remote work requires a connection capable of sustaining a video call and a file transfer at the
same time. Where your role includes live contribution, you are responsible for having a tested
fallback — a phone hotspot is sufficient — and for telling your lead when you do not.

### 5.3 Public networks

Working from a café, an airport or a hotel is working on an untrusted network and `INFOSEC` §4
applies in full. The VPN requirement in `INFOSEC` §4.1 is not waived because you are on holiday, on
a train, or in a hurry.

## 6. Hours, availability and time zones

### 6.1 Core hours

Staff working outside their home market's time zone are expected to be reachable for the four hours
that overlap their home market's 09:00–17:00 window, or to agree a different overlap with their
lead in writing before the arrangement starts. A three-hour time difference is workable; a
nine-hour difference usually is not, and that is the practical reason most long out-of-country
requests are declined rather than any of the tax reasons.

### 6.2 Remote work is not time off

A day worked from another location is a working day. It is not paid time off, it does not draw on
the `PTO` balance, and it is not an alternative to requesting leave. Combining the two — a week of
approved leave followed by two weeks working from the same place — is common and permitted, and
each part is requested under its own policy.

## 7. Ending or changing an arrangement

Westline may end an approved remote or multi-market arrangement with **10 business days'** notice
where the role's requirements change, where coverage cannot be sustained, or where a condition of
the approval — a security requirement, a payroll registration, an availability window — is not
being met. A staff member may end an arrangement at any time by returning to their home market.

Where an arrangement is ended for cause under `CONDUCT`, notice is not required.

## 8. Worked examples

These examples apply the rules in §1 to §4 to situations that come up repeatedly. Where an example
disagrees with those sections, those sections govern.

### 8.1 Two weeks from a cabin in British Columbia, for a Calgary producer

Outside the home province, fourteen days, inside Canada. This is §3.1: four weeks or less, so the
newsroom lead signs off and it is recorded in the scheduling system. No People & Culture review, no
Finance review. The lead is also implicitly waiving the three in-office days for that period under
§1.2, which is worth saying out loud when asking. Connectivity is the producer's responsibility under
§5.2, and if the cabin's connection cannot sustain a live contribution, that is a reason to decline
the arrangement rather than a detail to discover on air.

### 8.2 Six weeks from Lisbon, for a Vancouver host

Two separate thresholds are crossed at once, and both matter.

It is **more than four weeks**, so §3.2 requires People & Culture and Finance review. It is **outside
Canada**, so §3.3 requires that review regardless of duration — six weeks' notice, no self-approved
tier, and no lead sign-off that substitutes for it. The two rules are not alternatives; the
out-of-country rule alone would have applied to a single day.

Three other documents attach:

- `EXPENSE` §8.3 — some jurisdictions attach a withholding or filing obligation from the first day of
  work performed there, and extended presence can create a permanent-establishment exposure for
  Westline that an individual cannot consent to on the company's behalf.
- `BENEFITS` §5.3 — emergency travel medical covers the first 90 consecutive days of a trip outside
  the province. Six weeks is inside that, so coverage holds; a fourteen-week version of this request
  would not, and supplementary cover would have to be arranged.
- `INFOSEC` §6.1 — a **separate** notification to Information Security at least five business days
  before travelling with a work device, plus a travel device under §6.2. Doing the People & Culture
  and Finance approval does not do the security notification, and the security notification applies
  even to a holiday if a work device is going along.

The time-zone question in §6.1 is usually what decides it: Lisbon is eight or nine hours ahead of
Vancouver, so the four-hour overlap with the home market's working day cannot be met without one side
working through the night. Agreeing a different overlap in writing before departure is required.

Six weeks is also inside the eight-week annual cap in §3.4, but it consumes most of it — a second
out-of-country request later the same year would be declined with no market director override.

### 8.3 A day of email from Seattle after a weekend away

Still outside Canada, still §3.3, still requires approval before departure. There is no de minimis
tier and this is the case people are most surprised by. The honest guidance is either to arrange the
approval in advance, which for a single planned day is straightforward, or not to work.

### 8.4 Three months in the Vancouver office, for a Winnipeg staff member

Both are Westline offices, so §2.1 looks like the answer — notify the lead five business days ahead,
book a desk. But the stay exceeds four consecutive weeks in a **different province**, so §2.3 routes
it to §3.2: People & Culture and Finance review, six weeks ahead. Two Westline offices does not make
it an internal matter; payroll follows where the person physically is.

### 8.5 Moving permanently to another market

Not a remote-work question at all. A change of home market under §1.1 is a change to terms of
employment: payroll province, statutory holidays under `PTO` §5.1, per diem rate under `EXPENSE`
§3.2, and normally a compensation review against the new market's band. It goes to People & Culture
as a change request, not as a remote-work request, and it is not accomplished by working from the new
city for long enough.

### 8.6 A creator partner asking where they may work

`REMOTE` does not apply to creator partners at all — `HANDBOOK` §2 marks it `None`. Creator partners
choose their own place of work under their own agreement. `INFOSEC` and `SAFETY` still bind them
wherever they are, and `INFOSEC` §6 still governs a border crossing with a device holding Westline
material.
