# Westline Employee Handbook

## 1. About this handbook

### 1.1 Who Westline is

Westline Media Inc. builds, operates and manages a network of local news and entertainment
properties across Western Canada. We publish in six markets: Calgary (head office), Edmonton,
Vancouver, Kelowna, Saskatoon and Winnipeg. Our newsrooms are staffed by employees and supported
by a network of independent contractors and creator partners. Roughly 180 people are employed
directly, about 40 contractors are active in any given quarter, and around 250 creator partners
hold a current Creator Partner Agreement.

Westline's commercial model is advertising, and a large share of that advertising is produced
work: sponsored launches, demonstrations and features made to be as watchable as the editorial
around them. That model is why our disclosure rules in `EDITORIAL` are stricter than the legal
minimum, and why they apply to everyone who publishes under a Westline banner regardless of how
they are engaged.

### 1.2 What this handbook is, and is not

This handbook is the index to Westline's policies. It states which policies exist, who each one
binds, and where to go when a question crosses more than one of them. It does not restate the
policies themselves. Where this handbook and a specific policy document disagree, the specific
policy document governs, and you should tell People & Culture so we can fix the handbook.

Nothing in this handbook is an employment contract, and nothing in it varies the terms of an
individual employment agreement, contractor agreement or Creator Partner Agreement. Where a
signed agreement is more generous than policy, the agreement applies.

### 1.3 The three workforce classes

Almost every question about "does this apply to me" is answered by knowing which of three classes
you belong to. Your class is recorded in the people directory and does not change without a new
agreement.

**Staff** are employees of Westline Media Inc., salaried or hourly. Producers, newsroom leads,
hosts, reporters, sales, digital, finance and People & Culture are all staff. Staff are covered by
the full handbook.

**Contractors** are independent businesses engaged under a services contract: shooters, editors,
stringers, translators, audio operators. Contractors are covered by the conduct, safety, security,
editorial and social policies because those protect our audiences and sources, and by the parts of
the expense and onboarding policies that describe how contractors are paid and provisioned.
Contractors do not accrue paid time off, are not enrolled in benefits, and are not covered by the
leave or performance-review policies.

**Creator partners** are influencers and citizen journalists who publish under a Creator Partner
Agreement. They are not employees and not contractors. Their primary rulebook is `CREATOR`, which
also tells them which parts of the general policies bind them. Creator partners do not accrue paid
time off — the equivalent concept is the availability window described in `CREATOR` §5 — and are
not enrolled in Westline benefits, with the single exception of the Employee & Family Assistance
Program described in `BENEFITS` §6.

### 2.1 How to read a `Partial` row

A `Partial` row means the named sections bind you and the rest of that document does not. It is
not a suggestion to read the rest for context. Sections outside your class are frequently written
for a different commercial relationship and following them will give you the wrong answer. The
clearest example is `EXPENSE`: §5 sets equipment purchase limits for staff, and a creator partner
who read §5 would conclude that Westline buys their camera. It does not. `EXPENSE` §7 is the only
section of that document that applies to creator partners, and it says the opposite.

### 2.2 When two policies both apply and disagree

Some questions land on two policies at once, and the answer is a combination rather than a choice.
Working from Lisbon for six weeks is simultaneously a `REMOTE` question (duration and approval),
an `INFOSEC` question (crossing a border with a device that can reach newsroom systems) and an
`EXPENSE` question (payroll and tax consequences of working outside your home province). None of
those three documents overrides the others; all three conditions have to be satisfied.

Where two policies genuinely conflict — the same fact, two incompatible rules — the stricter rule
applies until People & Culture resolves it, and you should report the conflict.

## 3. How to use this handbook

### 3.1 Start with your class, then the policy

Look yourself up in §2, read the row for the policy you care about, then read the named sections
of that policy. If the row says `None`, stop: that policy is not your answer, and the note column
tells you where your answer actually lives.

### 3.2 Section numbering and citation

Every Westline policy uses stable numbered sections. A citation is written as the document id and
the section number — `PTO` §3.2, `EDITORIAL` §4.1 — and those numbers do not change between minor
versions. If a section is retired, its number is retired with it rather than reused.

### 3.3 Versions and effective dates

Every document carries a version and an effective date in its header. Policy applies as of its
effective date; it is not applied retroactively to a request that was already approved. Where a
change reduces an entitlement, we give at least 30 days' notice before the effective date.

## 5. Asking a question, and escalation

### 5.1 The normal route

Most questions go to your lead first: your newsroom lead if you are staff, your engaging producer
if you are a contractor, your program manager if you are a creator partner. Leads escalate to the
owner named in §4 when the answer is not obvious from the policy.

### 5.2 When to skip your lead

Go straight to a People & Culture partner, and do not raise it with your lead first, if your
concern is about your lead, if it involves harassment or discrimination, or if you believe raising
it through the normal route would expose you to retaliation. `CONDUCT` §5 describes the process
and `CONDUCT` §7 names the escalation path.

Safety incidents go to the Field Safety desk immediately and are reported within four hours under
`SAFETY` §7, regardless of who else is told. Suspected security incidents go to Information
Security within two hours under `INFOSEC` §7. Neither of those clocks waits for your lead to be
available.

### 5.3 What we will not answer through policy

Individual compensation outcomes, the result of a specific performance review, and anything under
an active investigation are not answered by pointing at a policy. `PERF` describes the process and
the calendar for reviews and says explicitly that it does not describe outcomes.

## 6. Changes to this handbook

This handbook is reviewed every January. Between reviews, changes are published with a new minor
version and a note in the market newsletters. Material reductions in entitlement are communicated
directly to affected people at least 30 days before they take effect.

## 7. Glossary

**Acting person.** The person a question is being asked on behalf of. Every answer in this handbook
depends on who is asking, because the applicability matrix in §2 is keyed to workforce class.

**Assignment desk.** The function that dispatches field work in each market and holds the check-in
records required by `SAFETY` §1.1.

**Availability window.** The creator partner equivalent of time away. Creator partners do not accrue
paid time off; they give notice of periods when they are unavailable for commissions. `CREATOR` §5.

**Blackout window.** A period in which paid time off is not normally approved in an affected market
because coverage demand is at its annual peak. `PTO` §4.

**Commissioning brief.** The written instruction that creates a paid deliverable from a creator
partner, including the rate reference and any production allowance. `CREATOR` §3.1.

**Creator partner.** An independent creator publishing under a Creator Partner Agreement. Not an
employee and not a contractor. `CREATOR` §1.

**Elimination period.** The unpaid interval between the start of a medical absence and the first
short-term disability payment. Seven calendar days. `LEAVE` §4.2.

**Home market.** The market a staff member is attached to, which sets statutory holidays, per diem
rate, payroll province and in-office expectation. `REMOTE` §1.1.

**Production allowance.** A budgeted amount, agreed in the commissioning brief before a shoot, for
costs specific to that segment. It is not a reimbursement scheme and it never covers equipment
purchases. `CREATOR` §4.1, `EXPENSE` §7.3.

**Restricted material.** Source material and anything else under `INFOSEC` §3. Never leaves the
secure store and never crosses an international border.

**Scope.** What a person may see about other people: `self`, `manager` or `hr_partner`. Distinct from
workforce class, which is what policy applies *to* them. §3 below.

**Tier.** For staff, the paid-time-off accrual band set by length of service (`PTO` §1.1). For creator
partners, the rate band set by Creator Partnerships (`CREATOR` §1.3). The two are unrelated and the
word is unfortunately reused.

**Waiting period.** The 90 days of continuous service before group benefits begin. `BENEFITS` §2.2.

**Workforce class.** One of `staff`, `contractor`, `creator_partner`. The single most important fact
about any policy question. §1.3.

## 8. Common questions, by class

These are the questions People & Culture is asked most often, with the document that answers each.
They are shortcuts to the right page, not answers in themselves.

### 8.1 If you are staff

*How much time off do I have, and how much notice do I need?* Entitlement in `PTO` §1.1, notice in
`PTO` §3 — 48 hours for one or two days, two weeks for three to five days, four weeks beyond that.

*Can I carry unused days into next year?* Up to five, used by March 31. `PTO` §2.1.

*When do my benefits start?* The first of the month after 90 days. Nothing is covered before then,
dental included. `BENEFITS` §2.2.

*Can I work from another city for a few weeks?* Inside your province, tell your lead. Outside your
province up to four weeks, your lead signs off. Longer than that, or outside Canada at all, People &
Culture and Finance review it six weeks ahead. `REMOTE` §3.

*I need a longer absence for a medical or family reason.* That is a leave of absence, not time off,
and it goes to a People & Culture partner rather than your lead. `LEAVE` §1.1.

*Will I get an increase this year?* `PERF` describes the process and the calendar and says explicitly
that it does not describe outcomes. Ask your lead at the mid-year check-in. `PERF` §1.

### 8.2 If you are a contractor

*What can I put on my invoice?* `EXPENSE` §6.1 lists it. Pre-approved assignment travel, per diems on
travel days, mileage, parking, accreditation and permits.

*Can I claim a lens or a laptop?* No. Equipment is your own capital cost, recovered through your day
rate. `EXPENSE` §6.2.

*Do I accrue vacation?* No. `PTO` does not apply to contractors; you invoice for days worked.

*Am I covered by the safety policy?* Yes, in full, including the risk assessment and incident rules.
`SAFETY` applies to everyone in the field.

*Can I use the assistance programme?* Yes, from day one, and Westline never learns you used it.
`BENEFITS` §6.

### 8.3 If you are a creator partner

*Can I expense equipment I bought for a shoot?* No. What you buy is yours and is not reimbursable.
`CREATOR` §7.1 and `EXPENSE` §7.2. If the brief needs kit you do not own, book a loaner (`CREATOR`
§7.2) or budget a rental in the production allowance before the shoot (`EXPENSE` §7.3).

*What can I invoice?* The base segment rate for your tier, plus any production allowance the
commissioning brief budgeted before the shoot. `CREATOR` §4.1.

*When do I get paid?* Net 30 from a complete invoice, submitted within 45 days of delivery.
`CREATOR` §4.3.

*Do I get vacation?* No. Give Creator Partnerships ten business days' notice of any unavailability
longer than seven days. `CREATOR` §5.3.

*Can I take a brand deal outside Westline?* Yes, with ten days' written notice and disclosure to our
standard, subject to any live category exclusivity window. `CREATOR` §6.

*Do I have to disclose sponsorship?* Yes, to the same measurable standard as staff: on screen in the
first five seconds, in the first two lines of the caption, spoken within thirty seconds for audio.
`EDITORIAL` §4.

*Can I read the staff vacation policy?* No — `PTO` is a staff document and is not available to
creator partners. It would not answer your question anyway; `CREATOR` §5 does.
