Westline Benefits Guide

This guide describes the Westline group benefits plan. It applies to staff. Contractors and creator partners are not enrolled in the group plan, with one exception: the Employee & Family Assistance Program in §6 is open to everyone engaged by Westline. See HANDBOOK §2.

This guide summarises the plan. Where it differs from the insurer's master policy or the benefits booklet, the master policy governs.

2. Eligibility

2.1 Who is eligible

Staff regularly scheduled for at least 20 hours per week on a permanent or fixed-term basis of at least six months are eligible. This is the same 20-hour threshold used for paid-time-off accrual in PTO §1.2.

Not eligible:

  • staff regularly scheduled below 20 hours per week;
  • staff on a fixed term shorter than six months, including most summer and semester internships;
  • contractors and creator partners, who are not employees of Westline.

An intern on a term shorter than six months is not eligible on either count and does not begin the waiting period. An intern whose term is extended past six months becomes eligible and the waiting period is counted from the original start date, not the extension date.

2.2 The waiting period

Eligible staff join the plan on the first day of the month following 90 days of continuous service. A staff member who starts on March 3 completes 90 days on June 1 and is covered from July 1.

There is no coverage of any kind under the group plan during the waiting period. This includes dental, which is the most common thing people assume is available on day one because a dental appointment is usually booked months ahead. It is not. A claim incurred during the waiting period is not payable even if it is submitted after coverage begins — the plan pays on the date of service, not the date of the claim.

The Employee & Family Assistance Program in §6 has no waiting period and is available from day one. It is the only benefit that is.

2.3 Waiving the waiting period

The waiting period is waived for a staff member who moves directly from a Westline contractor or creator partner engagement of at least 12 months into a staff role, and for a staff member rehired within 6 months of leaving. It is not waived on request and not waived for a pre-booked appointment.

3. Enrolment windows

3.1 Initial enrolment

You have 31 days from the date coverage begins to enrol dependants and to waive dental and vision if you are covered under a spouse's plan. Miss the window and the next opportunity is open enrolment, unless you have a life event under §3.3.

3.2 Annual open enrolment

Open enrolment runs November 1 to November 30 each year for coverage effective January 1. Changes made outside a life event take effect only at open enrolment.

3.3 Life events

A life event reopens enrolment for 31 days from the date of the event. Qualifying events: marriage or beginning a common-law relationship, separation or divorce, birth or adoption, death of a dependant, a dependant losing other coverage, and a change in a spouse's employment that gains or loses coverage.

Report life events within 31 days. Reported late, the change waits for open enrolment, and a newborn added late has no coverage in the interim.

4.2 Dental

Basic and preventive at 80%, major restorative at 50%, orthodontics at 50% to a $2,500 lifetime maximum per person. Combined annual maximum of $2,000 per person for basic and major. Recall examinations are covered every 9 months for adults and every 6 months for dependants under 18.

4.3 Long-term disability

Long-term disability begins after the 17-week short-term period described in LEAVE §4.2 and pays 60% of monthly base earnings to a maximum of $9,000 per month. Approval is the insurer's decision, not Westline's. Premiums are paid entirely by the staff member so that any benefit received is non-taxable.

4.4 Health spending account

$750 per plan year, credited January 1 or on joining the plan, pro-rated by remaining months in the first year. Unused credits carry forward one year and are then forfeited.

5. Limits and continuation

5.1 Dependants

A dependant is a spouse or common-law partner, and a child under 21, or under 25 if in full-time education, or of any age if disabled and dependent.

5.2 Pre-existing conditions

There is no pre-existing-condition exclusion on extended health or dental. Long-term disability carries a 12-month pre-existing-condition limitation for staff who join the plan after their initial eligibility date.

5.3 Travel and out-of-country coverage

Emergency travel medical covers the first 90 consecutive days of any trip outside your province of residence, including outside Canada, to $5,000,000 per incident. It covers emergencies only. It does not cover planned treatment, and it does not cover routine care while living abroad.

A trip longer than 90 consecutive days is not covered beyond day 90, and coverage does not restart by re-entering Canada briefly. Staff approved to work outside Canada under REMOTE §3.3 for longer than 90 days must arrange supplementary coverage; the People & Culture and Finance review required by that section includes this check. This is one of the substantive reasons a long out-of-country arrangement is reviewed rather than self-approved.

5.4 Continuation during a leave of absence

Westline pays its share of premiums during any paid leave and during the first 17 weeks of any unpaid leave, including parental leave. Beyond 17 weeks of unpaid leave, coverage continues only if the staff member pays both shares, arranged in advance. Coverage that lapses for non-payment is reinstated on return with no new waiting period. See LEAVE §6.3.

5.5 Coverage after employment ends

Group coverage ends on the last day of the month in which employment ends. Life insurance may be converted to an individual policy without evidence of insurability if applied for within 31 days. Extended health and dental cannot be converted.

6. Employee & Family Assistance Program

The Employee & Family Assistance Program is confidential counselling, crisis support, legal and financial consultation, and trauma response, delivered by an external provider. Westline receives aggregate usage statistics only and never learns who has used it.

The programme is open to everyone engaged by Westline — staff, contractors and creator partners alike — together with their household members, from the first day of the engagement. There is no waiting period and no enrolment step. This is deliberate: field work exposes contractors and creator partners to the same incidents as staff, and SAFETY §7.4 directs anyone involved in a traumatic incident here regardless of how they are engaged.

Counselling is short-term, at up to 8 sessions per issue per year, with referral onward where longer-term care is needed. Trauma response after a critical incident is arranged by the Field Safety desk and is not counted against the session limit.

7. Privacy of benefits information

Benefits information is medical information. It is held by People & Culture and the insurer, and it is not shared with your newsroom lead, your market director, or anyone else in your reporting line.

A manager can see the paid-time-off balance of a person who reports to them, because scheduling requires it (PTO §6.3). A manager cannot see whether that person is enrolled, what they are enrolled in, whether they have cleared the waiting period, or anything about a claim. A manager who needs to know whether an absence is covered asks a People & Culture partner, who answers the scheduling question without disclosing the medical one.

Staff may ask about their own coverage at any time. Questions about someone else's coverage go to a People & Culture partner and are answered only where that partner is satisfied there is a legitimate need.

8. Claims

8.1 How to claim

Most practitioners bill the insurer directly. Where they do not, submit through the insurer's app or portal with an itemised receipt showing the practitioner, the service, the date and the amount. Claims must be submitted within 12 months of the date of service, and by March 31 of the following year for a service in the final quarter.

8.2 Pre-determination

For dental work expected to exceed $500, ask the provider to submit a pre-determination before treatment. The insurer confirms what is payable, which avoids the common surprise of a major restorative procedure reimbursed at 50% rather than 80%.

8.3 Coordination of benefits

Where a staff member is also covered under a spouse's plan, claim against your own plan first and submit the unpaid balance to the other. For dependants, claim first against the plan of the parent whose birthday falls earlier in the calendar year. Coordinated coverage can reach 100% of an eligible expense but never more.

8.4 A declined claim

Declines are the insurer's decision and are appealed to the insurer, not to Westline. People & Culture can explain the plan terms and can ask the insurer to review a process failure; it cannot overturn an adjudication, and it does not see the clinical detail of a claim.

9. Worked examples

9.1 An intern who wants a dental cleaning

An intern on a four-month term in Edmonton asks whether dental is covered. Two things are true and either is sufficient. Under §2.1 a fixed term shorter than six months is not eligible at all, so the waiting period has not even started. And under §2.2 there is no coverage of any kind during the first 90 days regardless, dental included, because the plan pays on the date of service rather than the date of the claim.

What is available from day one is the assistance programme in §6. If the term is later extended past six months, eligibility begins and the 90 days is counted from the original start date, not from the extension.

9.2 A staff member 80 days in with a booked appointment

Coverage begins the first day of the month following 90 days of service, so an appointment on day 80 is not covered and an appointment on day 95 may still not be, if the first of the month has not yet arrived. The waiting period is not waived for a pre-booked appointment (§2.3). The practical advice is to move the appointment rather than to ask for an exception.

9.3 A new baby reported six weeks later

A birth is a life event under §3.3 and reopens enrolment for 31 days from the date of the event. Reported at six weeks, the window has closed, the child cannot be added until open enrolment in November, and there is no coverage for the child in the interim. This is the most costly of the common enrolment mistakes, and the deadline is the reason the onboarding session in ONBOARD §2 covers it.

9.4 A manager asking whether a report has benefits

Declined. Under §7 a manager can see a report's paid-time-off balance because scheduling requires it (PTO §6.3), and cannot see enrolment, waiting-period status or any claim. The legitimate route is a People & Culture partner, who will answer the scheduling question — when is this person expected back — without disclosing the medical one. This holds even when the manager's motive is entirely benign, because the restriction is about the information, not the intent.

9.5 A four-month assignment in Ireland

Emergency travel medical covers the first 90 consecutive days of a trip outside the province (§5.3). Four months exceeds that, coverage lapses on day 91, and it does not restart by flying home for a weekend. Supplementary coverage has to be arranged, and confirming that is part of the People & Culture and Finance review that REMOTE §3.3 requires before any out-of-country work.

9.6 A contractor asking about counselling

Contractors are not in the group plan, so extended health, dental and disability do not apply. The Employee & Family Assistance Program in §6 is open to them, and to creator partners, and to their household members, from day one with no enrolment step. After a critical incident the Field Safety desk arranges trauma response through the same programme regardless of class (SAFETY §7.4), and those sessions do not count against the annual limit.
