# Editorial Standards & Sponsored Content Disclosure

This document binds **everyone who publishes under a Westline banner** — staff, contractors and
creator partners alike, on Westline properties and on their own channels where the work is
Westline's. There is no partial application and no class exemption. See `HANDBOOK` §2.

## 1. Why this document is strict

Westline sells advertising that is made to be watched. Sponsored launches, demonstrations and
features are produced to the same craft standard as the editorial around them, by the same people,
on the same channels. That is the business, and it works only if the audience can always tell which
is which.

A viewer who cannot tell the difference has been misled, whether or not anyone intended it. So our
disclosure rules are written as measurable minimums — seconds, frame percentages, positions — rather
than as a principle to be interpreted. A rule you can measure is a rule you can check before you
publish.

Canadian advertising standards and the federal competition regime both require that material
connections be disclosed clearly and prominently. The rules in §4 are Westline's implementation of
that requirement, and they are deliberately stricter than the legal floor.

## 2. Accuracy

### 2.1 Verification

Every factual claim published under a Westline banner is verified before publication. A single
source is sufficient only for a matter of public record or a first-hand observation by the reporter.
Contested claims, allegations against a named person or organisation, and anything a reasonable
person would dispute require **two independent sources** or a document.

### 2.2 Attribution

Material from another outlet is attributed on first use, in the segment itself and not only in a
caption. Aggregation without attribution is plagiarism regardless of how the material was found.

### 2.3 Artificial intelligence

Generative tools may be used for research, transcription, translation drafts and rough edits.
Published output — text, voice, image or video — that was substantially generated rather than
recorded is labelled as such on screen. Synthetic voice or likeness of a real person is not published
without that person's written consent, without exception.

### 2.4 Archival and unpublishing

Published work is not silently altered. Substantive changes carry a dated correction note under §5.
Requests to unpublish are decided by the Editorial Standards desk and are granted only where
publication creates a demonstrable safety risk or where the material was published in error.

## 3. Fairness and independence

### 3.1 Right of reply

A person or organisation criticised in a segment is given a fair opportunity to respond before
publication, and the response, or the fact that no response was given, is included.

### 3.2 Sponsors do not touch editorial

A sponsor has no approval over, no advance sight of, and no influence on editorial coverage. A
sponsor's category is not a reason to run or to spike an editorial story. Sales does not see editorial
running orders before publication, and editorial does not see the sponsorship pipeline.

A sponsor **does** have approval over their own sponsored segment's brand elements — product
depiction, claims about their own product, brand marks. They do not have approval over the disclosure
in §4, and a request to remove or reduce it is refused and escalated to the Editorial Standards desk.

### 3.3 Payments, gifts and hospitality

Nobody publishing under a Westline banner accepts payment, gift or hospitality from the subject of
their coverage. Items sent for review are returned, donated, or declared and retained where return is
impractical, and their receipt is disclosed in the segment. Press-trip travel paid by a subject is
declined; where a trip is impossible on any other basis, it is disclosed prominently in the segment
itself and pre-approved by the Editorial Standards desk.

## 4. Sponsored content disclosure

### 4.1 What must be disclosed

Any material connection between the publisher and the subject is disclosed. That includes: a paid
sponsorship, a paid partnership, an affiliate arrangement, a free or loaned product retained beyond
review, a hosted trip, and any equity or family interest. If you had to think about whether it counts,
disclose it.

### 4.2 On-screen disclosure — video

Video segments carry an on-screen disclosure label meeting **all** of the following:

- **Appears within the first 5 seconds** of the segment;
- **On screen for at least 3 continuous seconds**;
- **At least 4% of frame height** for the label text;
- Positioned in the upper third or lower third, with sufficient contrast against the underlying
  image, never over a busy area of the frame;
- Reads `SPONSORED BY [BRAND]` or `PAID PARTNERSHIP WITH [BRAND]`. "In association with",
  "presented by", "supported by" and "thanks to" are **not** acceptable.

For segments longer than 5 minutes, the label reappears for 3 seconds at each subsequent 5-minute
mark. For a segment published as a series, every part carries its own disclosure; disclosing once in
part one is not sufficient.

### 4.3 Caption and description disclosure

Every sponsored post carries `#Sponsored` or `#WestlinePartner` **within the first two lines** of the
caption or description, before any "more" truncation on the platform in question. A tag placed after
a block of other hashtags, or below the fold, does not satisfy this rule.

### 4.4 Spoken disclosure — audio and audio-led video

Audio segments, podcasts and audio-led video carry a **spoken** disclosure within the **first 30
seconds**, in the host's own voice, naming the brand and the nature of the arrangement. A written
disclosure in the episode notes is required as well and does not replace the spoken one.

Live reads are disclosed immediately before the read begins, not at the end.

### 4.5 Sign-off before publication

Every sponsored segment is reviewed and signed off by the **Editorial Standards desk** before
publication, with a minimum of **3 business days** for the review. The review checks the disclosure
against §4.2 to §4.4, the accuracy of product claims against §2.1, and the absence of any editorial
influence under §3.2.

Sign-off is recorded against the commissioning brief. A segment published without sign-off is
unpublished until it is reviewed. For creator partners this is restated in `CREATOR` §3.2, and it
applies to the version that will actually go out, not to a rough cut.

### 4.6 Disclosure on personal channels

Where sponsored work made for Westline is cross-posted to a personal channel, the disclosure travels
with it, in that platform's native format, to the same standard. `SOCIAL` §4 covers personal channels
generally; for creator partners, `CREATOR` §6.2 extends the same standard to sponsored work made
for other brands entirely.

### 4.7 Common failures

The Editorial Standards desk sees the same five failures repeatedly. All of them are avoidable:

1. A disclosure that appears at the **end** of the segment. It must be in the first 5 seconds.
2. A caption tag pushed below the fold by other hashtags.
3. "Presented by" used in place of "Sponsored by" — see §4.2.
4. A multi-part series disclosed only in part one.
5. A vertical crop for a short-form platform that cuts the label out of frame. Check every aspect
   ratio you publish.

## 5. Corrections

### 5.1 Timing and placement

A correction is published within **24 hours** of the error being confirmed, on the same channel and
in the same format as the original: an on-screen correction for a video error, a spoken correction for
an audio error, a pinned note for a social post. A correction that lives only in a caption on a video
error is not a correction.

### 5.2 What a correction says

What was wrong, what is correct, and the date. It does not explain how the error happened and it does
not apportion blame within Westline.

### 5.3 Significant corrections

A correction that changes the substance of a story is additionally reported to the Editorial Standards
desk within 24 hours and logged in the corrections register, which is reviewed quarterly.

## 6. Conflicts of interest

### 6.1 Declaring

Declare to the Editorial Standards desk any relationship that a reasonable audience member would want
to know about when judging your work: paid or unpaid roles, financial interests, close personal
relationships with subjects, political candidacy or office, and paid work for any organisation you
cover. Declare it when it arises, not when it becomes a problem.

### 6.2 Managing

Most conflicts are managed by recusal from a specific subject rather than by ending the relationship.
The Editorial Standards desk decides; you do not decide your own conflict.

### 6.3 Outside work

Staff take outside paid media work only with written approval from their market director and the
Editorial Standards desk. Contractors and creator partners work for whoever they choose, subject to
disclosure under §6.1, to the exclusivity terms of their own agreements, and for creator partners to
the notice requirement in `CREATOR` §6.2.

## 7. Sources, legal exposure and escalation

### 7.1 Confidential sources

A promise of confidentiality to a source is absolute and binds Westline, not only the individual
journalist. Confidential source identities are recorded only where necessary, are held under
`INFOSEC` §3, and are never carried across an international border under `INFOSEC` §6.3.

### 7.2 Legal review

Segments alleging criminality, professional misconduct or dishonesty by a named party go to Legal
before publication, with a minimum of 2 business days.

### 7.3 Subpoenas and demands

Any subpoena, production order, takedown demand or law-enforcement request relating to Westline work
goes immediately to the Editorial Standards desk and to Legal, **before any response is given**,
including before confirming that material exists. Do not answer, do not hand anything over, and do not
delete anything. `LEAVE` §5.1 covers the employment side of a court attendance; this section covers
the editorial side, and this one comes first.

### 7.4 Escalation

Editorial concerns escalate to the Editorial Standards desk. A concern about pressure from a sponsor,
from sales, or from within editorial that you do not want to raise with your own lead goes directly to
the desk, and where it involves conduct, to a People & Culture partner under `CONDUCT` §5. Raising an
editorial concern in good faith is protected; retaliation for raising one is itself a `CONDUCT` breach.

## 8. Worked disclosure examples

The rules in §4 are measurable on purpose. These examples apply them to the formats Westline actually
publishes. Where an example disagrees with §4, §4 governs.

### 8.1 A three-minute sponsored product feature on the Westline site

A single label reading `SPONSORED BY [BRAND]` appears at 0:02, stays for 4 seconds, and is set at 5%
of frame height in the lower third against a clean part of the image. That satisfies §4.2 on all five
counts. The segment is under five minutes so no repeat is required. The caption opens
`#Sponsored — [brand] partnered with Westline to…`, putting the tag in the first two lines per §4.3.
Editorial Standards sign-off was requested six business days before publication, comfortably beyond
the three-day minimum in §4.5.

### 8.2 The same feature cut vertically for a short-form platform

The most common failure in §4.7. The vertical crop moves the safe area, and a lower-third label
positioned for 16:9 lands under the platform's own interface furniture or is cropped out entirely.
The disclosure has to be re-laid-out for every aspect ratio published, and checked in the platform's
own preview rather than in the edit. A label that is present in the master and invisible in the
published crop has not been published.

The platform's built-in "paid partnership" toggle is switched on as well, and under `SOCIAL` §5 that
toggle does not replace the caption tag, because it renders inconsistently across surfaces.

### 8.3 A four-part sponsored series

Every part carries its own disclosure in its own first five seconds. Disclosing in part one and
relying on the audience to remember does not satisfy §4.2, and part four is frequently the first one
a viewer sees.

### 8.4 A twelve-minute sponsored documentary

Longer than five minutes, so under §4.2 the label reappears for three seconds at 5:00 and again at
10:00, in addition to the opening label.

### 8.5 A podcast episode with a mid-roll live read

Two separate obligations. The episode itself carries a spoken disclosure in the host's own voice
within the first thirty seconds naming the brand and the arrangement (§4.4), plus a written disclosure
in the episode notes, which does not replace the spoken one. The mid-roll live read is additionally
disclosed immediately **before** the read begins, not after it — an unmarked read followed by "that
was our sponsor" has already misled the listener.

### 8.6 A camera sent for review and kept

A material connection under §4.1 even though no money changed hands. Under §3.3 it is returned,
donated, or declared and retained where return is impractical, and either way its receipt is disclosed
in the segment.

### 8.7 A creator partner's own brand deal, unrelated to Westline

`CREATOR` §6.2 applies the §4 standard to the creator partner's own channels. On-screen label in the
first five seconds, tag in the first two lines, spoken disclosure for audio. Ten days' written notice
to Creator Partnerships first, naming the brand and the product category, so that a live category
exclusivity window under `CREATOR` §6.1 can be checked. Notice is not permission-seeking; Westline can
tell a creator partner that a deal collides with a live window, and cannot tell them who to work with.

### 8.8 A sponsor asking for a smaller label

Refused, and escalated to the Editorial Standards desk under §3.2. The sponsor has approval over their
own brand elements and product claims; they have no approval over the disclosure. For a creator
partner, `CREATOR` §3.3 says the same thing and adds the practical instruction: do not negotiate it
yourself, refer them to Creator Partnerships.

### 8.9 A phrase that looks like disclosure and is not

"Presented by", "in association with", "supported by" and "thanks to" are explicitly not acceptable
under §4.2. They describe a relationship without naming it as paid, which is exactly the ambiguity the
rule exists to remove.
