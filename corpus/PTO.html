Paid Time Off & Statutory Holidays

This policy applies to Westline staff. Contractors and creator partners do not accrue paid time off — see HANDBOOK §2 for the applicability matrix and CREATOR §5 for the availability-window arrangement that replaces it for creator partners.

1. Entitlement

1.1 Annual entitlement by tenure

Paid time off is granted as a single annual bank covering vacation and personal days. Westline does not operate a separate sick-day allowance; short illness is taken from the same bank. Extended illness is a leave of absence and is covered by LEAVE §4, not by this policy.

Entitlement is set by completed years of continuous service as of January 1:

Tier 1Fewer than 2 years15 days1.25 days
Tier 22 years up to 5 years18 days1.50 days
Tier 35 years or more22 days1.833 days

A move between tiers takes effect on the first day of the month following the service anniversary. Entitlement is not pro-rated backwards for the part of the year already elapsed.

1.2 Part-time and variable-hours staff

Staff regularly scheduled for at least 20 hours per week accrue on the table above, pro-rated by scheduled hours against a 37.5-hour week. Staff regularly scheduled below 20 hours per week receive vacation pay at the statutory percentage for their province instead of an accrued day bank, and do not have a balance to draw against. The 20-hour threshold is the same threshold used for benefits eligibility in BENEFITS §2. Scheduled weekly hours themselves, and the breaks and rest that come with any shift, are set by HOURS §1.5.

1.3 Accrual during the first year

New staff begin accruing on their start date and may take accrued days from day one. Taking days in advance of accrual requires newsroom lead approval and is capped at 5 days. A negative balance at the end of employment is deducted from final pay where provincial law permits.

2. Carryover and payout

2.1 Carryover cap

A maximum of 5 unused days may be carried into the following calendar year. Anything above 5 days as of December 31 is forfeited. Carried days must be used by March 31; unused carried days are forfeited on April 1 and are not paid out.

Westline does not buy back paid time off. Accrued and unused days are paid out on termination of employment only, at the rate in effect on the last day worked.

2.2 When the cap can be waived

The carryover cap is waived, on written approval from a market director, where a request that would have used the days was declined by Westline for operational reasons, or where the staff member was on an approved leave of absence under LEAVE for more than 8 weeks in the accrual year. The waiver extends the March 31 deadline to June 30. It does not raise the annual entitlement.

3. Notice periods

Notice is counted in calendar days from the day the request is submitted in the scheduling system to the first day of leave requested. Notice requirements scale with the length of the request because longer absences require coverage to be built into the production schedule.

3–5 consecutive working days14 calendar days (2 weeks)Newsroom lead
More than 5 consecutive working days28 calendar days (4 weeks)Newsroom lead and market director

3.1 Requests of one to two days

Requests of one or two consecutive working days require 48 hours' notice and newsroom lead approval. This is the shortest notice the schedule can absorb without a coverage change.

3.2 Requests of three to five days

Requests of three to five consecutive working days require 14 calendar days' notice and newsroom lead approval. A request of this length usually means a shift swap or a freelance booking, and two weeks is the lead time our contractor pool works to.

A request submitted with less than 14 days' notice is not automatically declined. It is escalated to the market director, who may approve it where coverage already exists. Do not assume approval and do not book non-refundable travel before the request is approved in the scheduling system.

3.3 Requests of more than five days

Requests longer than five consecutive working days require 28 calendar days' notice and the approval of both the newsroom lead and the market director. Requests longer than 15 consecutive working days additionally require market director approval in writing and are reviewed against the blackout windows in §4 and the annual coverage plan.

3.4 Cancelling or changing an approved request

An approved request may be cancelled by the staff member at any time up to the first day of leave, and the days return to the balance. Westline may recall a staff member from approved leave only for a declared major news event, and where that happens the days are returned to the balance and any non-refundable costs are reimbursed under EXPENSE §3.

4. Blackout windows

4.1 What a blackout window is

A blackout window is a period in which paid time off is not normally approved in an affected market because coverage demand is at its annual peak. Requests overlapping a blackout window are declined unless the market director approves an exception in writing. A blackout window does not cancel an already-approved request.

4.2 Exceptions

Exceptions are granted for statutory leave under LEAVE, for bereavement, and for pre-existing commitments booked before the blackout calendar was published for that year. The blackout calendar is published each January 15 for the following twelve months.

5.2 Statutory holidays falling during paid time off

A statutory holiday falling inside an approved block of paid time off is not deducted from the balance. A five-working-day request spanning one statutory holiday consumes four days.

6. Requesting time off

6.1 How to request

All requests are submitted in the scheduling system. A request is not approved until it shows as approved there; a verbal agreement with a lead is not an approval. Where a request needs market director approval under §3.3, the scheduling system routes it automatically.

6.2 How requests are prioritised

Overlapping requests in the same team are resolved by submission timestamp, except that no individual is granted a peak-period request in two consecutive years where that would deny a colleague a first-time request for the same period.

6.3 Checking your balance

Your balance, accrual to date, days used and pending requests are visible in the scheduling system. Balances shown include approved-but-not-yet-taken days. Your newsroom lead can see the balance of anyone who reports to them; they cannot see benefits information, which is covered by BENEFITS §7.

7. Interaction with other policies

Paid time off does not run at the same time as a leave of absence. Where a bereavement or medical leave begins during an approved block of paid time off, the unused days return to the balance from the first day of the leave, as described in LEAVE §6.2. Paid time off continues to accrue during paid leave and during the first 30 days of unpaid leave, and stops accruing after that, as described in LEAVE §6.1.

Working from another location during approved paid time off is not paid time off. If you are working, you are working, and REMOTE applies.

One day of paid time off is one standard working day: 7.5 paid hours, as defined in HOURS §1.1. Part-time staff draw days pro-rated against the 37.5-hour week on the same basis. Time in lieu of overtime is not paid time off — it is banked and used under HOURS §7.3, it is not subject to the carryover cap in §2.1, and it is not paid out.

8. Worked examples

These examples are illustrative and use the figures in this policy as at its effective date. If a figure here disagrees with §1 to §5, those sections govern.

8.1 A three-day request, twelve days out

A producer with a balance of 11 days asks for the Tuesday, Wednesday and Thursday of a week twelve calendar days away. The balance covers it comfortably. The length is three consecutive working days, so §3.2 applies and the minimum notice is 14 calendar days — twelve is short. The request is not declined automatically; it escalates to the market director, who may approve it where coverage already exists. The correct advice is to submit it now, expect the escalation, and not book anything non-refundable until the scheduling system shows approval.

8.2 The same request during Stampede

The same three days falling between July 3 and July 13 for a Calgary or Edmonton staff member collide with the blackout window in §4.1. The notice question in §3.2 still applies, and the blackout question is a separate and stronger one: the request is declined unless the market director approves an exception in writing. A Vancouver staff member making the same request is unaffected, because that window names Calgary and Edmonton only.

8.3 A week off that contains a statutory holiday

A staff member takes the five working days of a week containing Victoria Day. Under §5.2 the statutory holiday is not deducted, so the week consumes four days, not five. The request is still five consecutive working days for the purposes of §3.2, so 14 days' notice applies: length of request and days consumed are two different questions.

8.4 Falling ill during approved time off

A staff member is ill for two days of an approved week. Short illness comes from the same bank under §1.1, so nothing changes and no days are returned. If the illness ran past five consecutive working days it would become a medical leave under LEAVE §4.2, and from the first day of that leave the remaining time-off days would return to the balance under LEAVE §6.2.

8.5 Carryover at year end

A staff member ends December with 8 unused days. Five carry into the new year and three are forfeited on December 31 under §2.1. The five carried days must be used by March 31 or they are forfeited on April 1 and are not paid out. If the reason the days went unused was a request Westline declined for operational reasons, §2.2 allows a market director to waive the cap and extend the deadline to June 30 — but that waiver has to be asked for.

8.6 A new starter who wants a week off in month two

A staff member two months in has accrued 2.5 days at Tier 1. A five-day request exceeds the balance. §1.3 permits taking days in advance of accrual with newsroom lead approval, capped at 5 days, so the request is possible. The notice requirement is unchanged at 14 calendar days, and a negative balance at the end of employment is recoverable from final pay where provincial law allows.

8.7 A manager checking a report's balance

A newsroom lead can see the balance, accrual, days used and pending requests of anyone who reports to them, because scheduling requires it (§6.3). The same lead cannot see that person's benefits enrolment, waiting period or claims — that is medical information and BENEFITS §7 restricts it to the individual and People & Culture. A lead who needs to know whether an absence is covered asks a People & Culture partner, who answers the scheduling question without disclosing the medical one.

9. Questions we are asked often

Does a half day count? Yes, in half-day units. A half day counts as a working day for the purposes of the notice thresholds in §3.

Can I take unpaid time instead? Unpaid time off is approved by a market director and is unusual where a balance exists. It does not have its own notice schedule; §3 applies by length.

Can I sell back unused days? No. Westline does not buy back paid time off, and accrued days are paid out only on termination. §2.1.

My lead said yes in the newsroom. Is that approval? No. A request is approved when the scheduling system says it is. §6.1.

I am a contractor. How does this work for me? It does not. Contractors do not accrue paid time off and invoice for days worked. See HANDBOOK §2.

I am a creator partner. What is my equivalent? The availability window in CREATOR §5. There is no balance, nothing to request, and ten business days' notice for any unavailability longer than seven days.
