# Field Safety & Live Coverage

This document binds everyone working in the field on Westline business — staff, contractors and
creator partners — including on an unpaid pitch you intend to sell to Westline afterwards. See
`HANDBOOK` §2.

Nothing in this document requires you to complete an assignment. **No story is worth an injury**, and
withdrawing from a situation is always an acceptable outcome that will never be held against you.

## 1. Before you go

### 1.1 The check-in record

Every field assignment has a check-in record filed with your assignment desk before you leave:
where you are going, who you expect to meet, when you expect to be clear, and a contact who is not on
the assignment. For a routine daytime shoot in your home market this is one line in the assignment
system. For anything in §2.2 it is a full risk assessment.

### 1.2 Kit

Take what the assignment needs: visible identification, a charged phone plus a power bank, water,
weather-appropriate clothing, and for any road assignment outside a city, a blanket and a shovel
between November and April. High-visibility outerwear is required for any roadside work.

### 1.3 Identification

Westline identification is carried and shown when asked. Do not misrepresent who you work for, and do
not conceal that you are recording except where the Editorial Standards desk has approved it in
advance under `EDITORIAL` §3.

## 2. Risk assessment

### 2.1 When one is required

A written risk assessment, approved by the Field Safety desk, is required before:

- any assignment at a protest, demonstration, picket line or crowd event;
- any assignment where violence has occurred at a comparable event in the past 12 months;
- any overnight or after-22:00 assignment outside a city centre;
- any assignment involving wildfire, flood, severe weather or an active police perimeter;
- any assignment outside Canada;
- any drone flight (§5);
- any assignment where the subject has previously threatened a journalist.

### 2.2 What it covers

The identified hazards, the mitigations, the withdrawal trigger — the specific, named condition on
which you leave — the communications plan, and the nearest medical facility. Withdrawal triggers are
decided before you go, not in the moment, because in the moment the calculus is worse.

### 2.3 Turnaround

Submit at least **2 business days** ahead for a planned assignment. Breaking news is assessed by
telephone with the Field Safety desk and the record is written up afterwards; the desk is staffed
06:00–23:00 daily and by pager outside those hours.

## 3. Working alone

### 3.1 Solo shooting

Solo shooting is normal for daytime assignments in populated areas. It is **not permitted** for:

- crowd events and protests;
- any assignment after **22:00** outside a city centre, unless a check-in protocol under §3.2 is
  agreed in advance;
- any assignment with an identified risk of violence;
- any assignment in a remote area without mobile coverage.

### 3.2 Check-in protocol

Where solo work is approved for a higher-risk assignment, a check-in protocol is agreed: an interval
(normally 30 minutes), a contact who is awake and expecting the calls, and an escalation action if a
check-in is missed. A missed check-in is escalated after **15 minutes**, and the escalation is a real
call to a real person, not a message.

### 3.3 Two-person assignments

Where a second person is required, that person is a Westline-engaged operator or producer, not a
friend, a partner or a member of the public.

## 4. Specific conditions

### 4.1 Crowds, protests and picket lines

Position at the edge with an unobstructed exit and identify a second exit. Do not enter a kettle or a
confined space. Wear identification visibly unless the desk has assessed that it increases risk, in
which case remove it entirely rather than concealing it. Withdraw when the police declare an unlawful
assembly, when the crowd's mood turns toward media, or when your exit is blocked — any one of those,
not all three.

### 4.2 Weather and road conditions

Do not drive through standing water, do not cross a closed road, and do not stop on a highway shoulder
for a shot. Winter travel outside a city requires a check of the provincial road authority before
departure and a check-in on arrival. If the assignment requires driving in conditions you would not
drive in personally, it does not require it.

### 4.3 Wildfire and flood

Only inside the perimeter that the incident commander has cleared media into, only with the mandated
protective equipment, and only with a named liaison. Never self-deploy inside an evacuation order.

### 4.4 Night coverage

After 22:00: park in lit areas, keep the vehicle between you and the road, do not display equipment
while walking, and tell the desk when you are clear. §3.1 restricts solo night work outside city
centres.

### 4.5 Live broadcast positions

A live position is assessed for traffic, crowd flow and overhead hazards before the truck is set. The
operator has final say on the position and may move or cancel it; a producer cannot overrule that, and
a lead who pushes back on a cancelled live position is in breach of this policy.

## 5. Drones and remotely piloted aircraft

### 5.1 Certification and registration

Anyone flying a drone on Westline business holds a valid **Transport Canada RPAS pilot certificate**
at the level the operation requires — Basic Operations or Advanced Operations — and the aircraft is
registered with Transport Canada and marked with its registration.

This applies identically to staff, contractors and creator partners, and it applies to a creator
partner's own drone. Equipment ownership is a `CREATOR` §7 question; certification is this one, and
owning the aircraft does not change what you need to fly it.

### 5.2 Advanced operations

An **Advanced Operations** certificate is required to fly in controlled airspace, within 30 metres of
people, or over people. Flight **over a crowd or an assembly of people** additionally requires a
Special Flight Operations Certificate from Transport Canada. Westline does not hold a blanket SFOC;
each one is applied for per operation and takes weeks, so a crowd overflight is planned long in
advance or not at all.

### 5.3 Airspace and site restrictions

Before every flight, check the current airspace picture, active NOTAMs, and site restrictions.
Flight is prohibited in controlled airspace without an RPAS flight authorisation, within 5.6 km of an
airport or 1.9 km of a heliport without authorisation, over or near an active emergency scene, within
a national park without a permit, and inside any temporary flight restriction, which is routinely
issued over wildfires. A drone launched near a wildfire grounds the water bombers; this is the single
most serious drone failure in Canadian news work.

### 5.4 Westline approval

Every drone flight on Westline business requires **Field Safety desk approval at least 5 business
days before the flight**, submitted with the certificate number, the aircraft registration, the
planned location and altitude, the airspace check, and proof of liability insurance of at least
**$2,000,000**. Breaking-news drone flights are approved by telephone and written up afterwards; a
convenient shot is not breaking news.

### 5.5 On the day

Maintain visual line of sight at all times. Do not fly beyond visual line of sight, at night without
the required lighting and certification, over moving vehicles, or in winds beyond the aircraft's
stated limit. A visual observer is required for any flight near people. Log every flight.

## 6. Equipment and vehicles

Inspect equipment before an assignment and remove damaged items from service rather than working
around them. Tripods and lighting stands are weighted outdoors. Batteries are transported in
fire-resistant bags. Vehicles are not loaded above the roofline and loose equipment is secured — a
camera on a back seat is a projectile in a collision.

Creator partners using loaner equipment under `CREATOR` §7.2 inspect it on collection and report
damage before use, not after.

## 7. Incidents

### 7.1 Report within four hours

Any of the following is reported to the Field Safety desk **within 4 hours**: an injury, however
minor; a threat or an assault; damage to a vehicle or to equipment; a near miss; police detention or
the seizure of equipment; a drone incident of any kind including a fly-away or an unplanned landing.

The four-hour clock does not wait for your lead to be available and does not wait until you are home.
Reporting is not an admission of fault and does not begin a disciplinary process.

### 7.2 Immediate steps

Get safe first. Call emergency services if anyone is hurt. Do not give a statement to anyone other
than emergency services or police until you have spoken to the Field Safety desk, and where the
incident involves seized material or a demand for footage, `EDITORIAL` §7.3 governs and you say
nothing about what material exists.

### 7.3 Investigation

Incidents are reviewed by the Field Safety desk within 5 business days and the review is about the
conditions, not the individual. Reviews are published to the safety register with names removed.

### 7.4 After a traumatic incident

Trauma response is arranged by the Field Safety desk through the Employee & Family Assistance
Program. It is available to **staff, contractors and creator partners alike** — `BENEFITS` §6 is open
to everyone engaged by Westline from day one, precisely so that this section can be class-blind — and
sessions arranged after a critical incident do not count against the annual session limit. Nobody at
Westline is told that you used it.

Where an incident involves harassment or assault by a colleague rather than by a member of the public,
`CONDUCT` §5 applies alongside this section and the two processes run in parallel.

## 8. Assignment playbooks

Short-form versions of the rules above, arranged by the assignment types that come up most. They do
not replace §1 to §7 and a risk assessment under §2 is still required where §2.1 says it is.

### 8.1 Daytime shoot in your own market

Check-in record filed (§1.1). Identification carried (§1.3). Solo is fine (§3.1). No risk assessment
required. Report anything that happens within four hours (§7.1).

### 8.2 Protest or picket line

Risk assessment required and approved before you go (§2.1). Not solo (§3.1). Position at the edge with
two identified exits; do not enter a confined space (§4.1). Withdrawal triggers agreed in advance:
police declaring an unlawful assembly, the crowd turning toward media, or your exit blocked — any one
of the three. Identification worn visibly, or removed entirely, never concealed.

### 8.3 Winter road assignment outside a city

Blanket and shovel between November and April (§1.2). Provincial road authority checked before
departure and a check-in on arrival (§4.2). No stopping on a highway shoulder for a shot; no driving
through standing water; no crossing a closed road. If you would not drive it personally, the
assignment does not require it.

### 8.4 Night coverage after 22:00 outside a city centre

Not solo unless a check-in protocol is agreed in advance (§3.1). Where it is: a 30-minute interval, a
named contact who is awake and expecting the calls, escalation after 15 minutes of silence, and the
escalation is a phone call to a person (§3.2). Park in lit areas, keep the vehicle between you and the
road, do not display equipment while walking, and tell the desk when you are clear (§4.4).

### 8.5 Wildfire

Risk assessment required (§2.1). Only inside the perimeter the incident commander has cleared media
into, only with the mandated protective equipment, only with a named liaison, never self-deployed
inside an evacuation order (§4.3). A temporary flight restriction is almost certainly in force, so a
drone is prohibited (§5.3) — this is the failure that grounds water bombers and it is the most serious
drone error in Canadian news work.

### 8.6 Drone flight

Certificate at the right level and aircraft registered and marked (§5.1). Advanced Operations for
controlled airspace, within 30 m of people, or over people; a Transport Canada Special Flight
Operations Certificate on top of that for flight over a crowd, which takes weeks and is planned far
ahead or not at all (§5.2). Airspace, NOTAMs and site restrictions checked on the day (§5.3). Field
Safety desk approval submitted **at least five business days before the flight**, with certificate
number, registration, location, altitude, airspace check and proof of $2,000,000 liability cover
(§5.4). Visual line of sight maintained; a visual observer for any flight near people; every flight
logged (§5.5).

Creator partners: identical requirements. Owning the drone is a `CREATOR` §7 question and changes
nothing here — the certificate, the registration, the airspace check, the five business days and the
insurance all still apply (`CREATOR` §7.4).

### 8.7 Assignment outside Canada

Risk assessment required regardless of how routine it looks (§2.1). `REMOTE` §3.3 approval from People
& Culture and Finance before departure. `INFOSEC` §6.1 notification to Information Security at least
five business days ahead, a travel device under §6.2, and under `INFOSEC` §6.3 no source material
crosses the border in any form.
