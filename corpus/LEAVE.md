# Leaves of Absence

This policy applies to Westline staff. It sets out Westline's leave entitlements, which meet or
exceed the statutory minimums in Alberta, British Columbia, Saskatchewan and Manitoba. Contractors
and creator partners are not covered; their statutory entitlements, where any exist, come from their
own employment status rather than from Westline. See `HANDBOOK` §2.

A leave of absence is a formal, dated absence from work with a defined start and expected end. It
is not the same thing as paid time off, and the two never run at the same time — see §6.2.

## 1. Requesting a leave

### 1.1 Who to tell, and when

Leave requests go to a People & Culture partner, not to your newsroom lead. Your lead is told the
dates so they can plan coverage; they are not told the reason, the medical detail, or anything
beyond what is needed to schedule. Where notice is possible, give **4 weeks** for a planned leave
and as much as you can for an unplanned one. Bereavement and emergency medical leave have no notice
requirement at all.

### 1.2 What we ask for

We ask for the expected start date, the expected duration, and where the leave type requires it, the
supporting document named in that section. We do not ask for a diagnosis, and a medical note that
states a diagnosis will be redacted before it is filed.

### 1.3 Confirmation

Every approved leave is confirmed in writing with a start date, an expected return date, the pay
treatment, and what happens to benefits and to paid-time-off accrual. If you do not have that letter,
your leave is not approved yet.

## 2. Parental leave

### 2.1 Pregnancy leave

A staff member who is pregnant is entitled to up to **17 weeks** of pregnancy leave, beginning no
earlier than 13 weeks before the expected due date. Pregnancy leave is job-protected and unpaid by
Westline; Employment Insurance maternity benefits apply where the staff member qualifies.

### 2.2 Parental leave

Each parent is entitled to parental leave following the birth or adoption of a child. Standard
parental leave is up to **61 weeks** for a staff member who has also taken pregnancy leave, and up to
**63 weeks** otherwise, to a combined maximum of **69 weeks** shared between two parents of the same
child. Parental leave must begin within 78 weeks of the birth or placement.

### 2.3 Westline top-up

Staff with **12 months** of continuous service at the start of leave receive a Westline top-up of
**75% of regular base pay for 8 weeks**, paid on the normal payroll cycle, on top of any Employment
Insurance benefit. The top-up is paid once per birth or placement, not once per parent per leave
period, and is shared if both parents are Westline staff.

The top-up is repayable in full if the staff member does not return to work for at least **6 months**
after the leave ends, except where the failure to return is itself the result of a medical leave, a
layoff, or a termination by Westline other than for cause.

### 2.4 Returning

Return is to the same position, or where that position no longer exists, to a comparable position in
the same market at not less than the same pay. A return date may be moved once, earlier or later, on
4 weeks' notice.

## 3. Bereavement leave

### 3.1 Entitlement

Bereavement leave is paid by Westline and is not deducted from the paid-time-off balance.

| Relationship | Paid days | Notice |
|---|---|---|
| Immediate family | 5 working days | None required |
| Extended family | 2 working days | None required |
| Pregnancy loss or stillbirth | 5 working days, either parent | None required |
| Chosen family, on request | Up to 3 working days | Confirm with a People & Culture partner |

"Immediate family" means a spouse or partner, a child or step-child, a parent or step-parent, a
sibling, a grandparent or a grandchild. "Extended family" means an aunt, uncle, niece, nephew,
cousin, or the equivalent relation of a spouse or partner. The "chosen family" line exists because
those two lists do not describe everyone's life, and a People & Culture partner will not ask you to
justify a relationship beyond naming it.

### 3.2 Timing and extension

Bereavement days need not be consecutive and may be taken within **12 months** of the death, which
matters when a service or an interment is months later. Additional unpaid time, or paid time drawn
from the paid-time-off balance, is approved on request.

### 3.3 Travel

Travel to a funeral is not a business expense and is not reimbursable under `EXPENSE`. Where the
death occurs while the staff member is travelling on Westline business, the cost of changing return
travel is reimbursed under `EXPENSE` §3.5.

## 4. Medical and disability leave

### 4.1 Short absences

Absence of **five consecutive working days or fewer** for illness is taken from the paid-time-off
balance under `PTO` §1.1 and is not a leave of absence. No medical note is required.

### 4.2 Short-term disability

Absence beyond five consecutive working days is medical leave. Short-term disability benefit begins
after an **elimination period of 7 calendar days** from the first day of absence and pays **70% of
regular base pay for up to 17 weeks**. It requires an attending physician's statement confirming
that you are unable to work and giving an expected duration; it does not require a diagnosis.

### 4.3 Long-term disability

Long-term disability is a benefits-carrier decision, not a Westline decision. It begins after the
17-week short-term period and is described in `BENEFITS` §4.3. Eligibility requires enrolment in the
plan, which requires having cleared the waiting period in `BENEFITS` §2.2 — a staff member still in
the waiting period has no long-term disability coverage.

### 4.4 Graduated return

A graduated return to work is arranged where the attending physician recommends one, typically over
2 to 8 weeks. Pay during a graduated return is pro-rated to hours worked, topped up by the
short-term disability benefit for the balance. The reduced schedule replaces the standard week in
`HOURS` §1.1 for the period it runs, and is published like any other schedule under `HOURS` §5.1 so
that the desk does not roster hours the return plan does not allow.

## 5. Other statutory leaves

### 5.1 Jury duty and court attendance

Leave for jury duty or for attendance as a subpoenaed witness is paid: Westline pays the difference
between any court allowance received and regular base pay, for the duration of service. Forward the
summons to a People & Culture partner. Attendance as a witness in a matter where you are a party is
unpaid, and is taken from the paid-time-off balance or as unpaid leave.

Journalists served with a subpoena relating to Westline work must notify the Editorial Standards
desk and Legal **before responding**, under `EDITORIAL` §7.3 and `INFOSEC` §3.4. Source protection
obligations do not pause because a court is involved.

### 5.2 Compassionate care and critical illness

Up to **27 weeks** of unpaid, job-protected leave to care for a gravely ill family member with a
significant risk of death within 26 weeks, and up to **37 weeks** to care for a critically ill child.
Both require a medical certificate and both may be taken in blocks.

### 5.3 Domestic and sexual violence leave

Up to **10 days** — the first **5 of them paid** — plus up to 15 further weeks unpaid, in a
52-week period. Requests are handled by a named People & Culture partner, are not recorded in the
scheduling system with a reason, and are not disclosed to the staff member's lead beyond the dates.

### 5.4 Reservist leave

Unpaid, job-protected leave for Canadian Forces reserve service and training, on 4 weeks' notice
where the deployment allows it.

## 6. How leave interacts with other policies

### 6.1 Paid-time-off accrual during leave

Paid time off continues to accrue at the normal rate during any **paid** leave, and during the first
**30 calendar days** of any unpaid leave. Accrual stops on day 31 of unpaid leave and restarts on the
day you return. Accrual that stopped is not backfilled on return.

Where an unpaid leave of more than 8 weeks prevented a staff member from using their entitlement,
the carryover cap in `PTO` §2.1 may be waived under `PTO` §2.2.

### 6.2 A leave beginning during approved paid time off

Paid time off and a leave of absence never run at the same time. Where a leave begins during an
approved block of paid time off — a bereavement in the middle of a vacation is the common case — the
paid-time-off days from the first day of the leave onward are **returned to the balance**, and the
absence is recorded as leave from that date. This is not automatic: tell a People & Culture partner,
because the scheduling system cannot detect it.

The reverse does not apply. Paid time off requested to extend the end of a leave is a new request and
is subject to the notice periods in `PTO` §3.

### 6.3 Benefits continuation during leave

Westline continues to pay its share of benefit premiums for the full duration of any paid leave, and
for the first **17 weeks** of any unpaid leave including parental leave. Beyond 17 weeks of unpaid
leave, coverage continues only if the staff member pays both shares, arranged in advance with a
People & Culture partner. Coverage that lapses for non-payment is reinstated on return without a new
waiting period. `BENEFITS` §5.4 describes the mechanics.

### 6.4 Statutory holidays during leave

Statutory holidays falling during a **paid** leave are paid as normal. Statutory holidays falling
during an **unpaid** leave are not paid.

### 6.5 Performance review during leave

A staff member on leave for more than 8 weeks of a review period is reviewed on the portion of the
period actually worked, and the absence is not itself a performance factor. `PERF` §5.2.

## 7. Returning to work

Confirm your return date with a People & Culture partner at least **2 weeks** in advance so that
system access can be restored — access is suspended, not deleted, during leaves longer than 8 weeks
under `ONBOARD` §6.3 — and so that your lead can put you back on the schedule. Equipment retained
during leave does not need to be returned unless the leave exceeds 26 weeks.

## 8. Worked examples

### 8.1 A death in the family during approved time off

A staff member is three days into an approved week of paid time off when a parent dies. Bereavement
leave under §3.1 is five paid working days for immediate family, it is not deducted from the
paid-time-off balance, and it requires no notice.

Under §6.2, the paid-time-off days from the first day of the bereavement leave onward return to the
balance, and the absence is recorded as leave from that date. The two remaining days of the approved
week come back; the three already taken do not. This does not happen automatically — the scheduling
system cannot detect it — so a People & Culture partner has to be told.

Under §3.2 the five days need not be consecutive and may be taken within twelve months, which matters
when the service is weeks later. Additional time is available, either unpaid or drawn from the
paid-time-off balance. Travel to the funeral is not reimbursable (§3.3, `EXPENSE` §3.5).

### 8.2 An illness that outgrows the time-off bank

A staff member is off sick for four days, then a fifth, then a physician signs them off for a further
three weeks. The first five consecutive working days come from the paid-time-off bank under §4.1 and
are not a leave of absence. From day six it is medical leave. Short-term disability begins after the
seven-calendar-day elimination period in §4.2, so days six and seven are unpaid unless covered by the
time-off balance, and the benefit then pays 70% of base pay for up to seventeen weeks.

Paid-time-off accrual continues throughout, because short-term disability is paid leave under §6.1.
Benefit premiums continue under §6.3.

### 8.3 A staff member still in the benefits waiting period

The same illness, for a staff member sixty days into the job. Short-term disability is a benefit under
the group plan, and there is no coverage of any kind during the ninety-day waiting period
(`BENEFITS` §2.2). There is no short-term disability, and no long-term disability either — §4.3 is
explicit that eligibility requires enrolment, which requires having cleared the waiting period.

What exists instead: the paid-time-off bank, any advance days a lead approves under `PTO` §1.3, and
the Employee & Family Assistance Program, which has no waiting period at all (`BENEFITS` §6). This is
the case where the correct answer is a People & Culture conversation rather than a policy citation.

### 8.4 Parental leave for two Westline parents

Both parents are Westline staff and both have more than twelve months' service. Each takes parental
leave, sharing the combined 69-week maximum in §2.2. The Westline top-up in §2.3 is paid **once per
birth**, not once per parent, so it is 75% of base pay for eight weeks, shared between them rather
than doubled. Benefit premiums continue at Westline's share for the first seventeen weeks of each
parent's unpaid leave under §6.3; beyond that, each pays both shares to keep coverage.

If one parent leaves Westline within six months of returning, their portion of the top-up is
repayable under §2.3, unless the reason for not returning is itself a medical leave, a layoff, or a
Westline termination other than for cause.

### 8.5 Jury duty in a matter involving a Westline story

Jury service is paid under §5.1 — Westline pays the difference between the court allowance and base
pay. But where the summons or subpoena relates to Westline work, `EDITORIAL` §7.3 comes first: the
Editorial Standards desk and Legal are told **before any response is given**, including before
confirming that material exists. Source protection does not pause because a court is involved
(`INFOSEC` §3.4). The employment question and the editorial question are answered by different people
in different orders, and the editorial one is answered first.

## 9. Questions we are asked often

**Is a leave paid?** Bereavement and jury duty are paid by Westline. Medical leave is paid by the
short-term disability benefit after a seven-day elimination period. Parental leave is unpaid by
Westline, with the eight-week top-up in §2.3 on top of Employment Insurance. Compassionate care,
critical illness and reservist leave are unpaid.

**Do I have to use my time off first?** No. A leave of absence is not conditional on exhausting the
paid-time-off balance, and the two never run at the same time (§6.2).

**Will my lead be told why?** No. Your lead is told the dates. The reason stays with People & Culture
(§1.1), and for domestic and sexual violence leave the scheduling record carries no reason at all
(§5.3).

**Does my time off keep accruing?** During any paid leave, and during the first thirty calendar days
of any unpaid leave. It stops on day thirty-one and restarts when you return; the gap is not
backfilled (§6.1).

**What happens to my benefits?** Westline's share continues through any paid leave and the first
seventeen weeks of unpaid leave. Beyond that you pay both shares to keep coverage, arranged in advance
(§6.3, `BENEFITS` §5.4).

**Will this affect my review?** No. A staff member on leave for more than eight weeks of a cycle is
assessed on the portion actually worked, and the absence is never a performance factor (§6.5,
`PERF` §5.2).

**I am a contractor or a creator partner. Does any of this apply?** No. `LEAVE` is a staff policy
(`HANDBOOK` §2). Creator partners give notice of unavailability under `CREATOR` §5.3. Everyone,
in every class, can use the assistance programme in `BENEFITS` §6.
