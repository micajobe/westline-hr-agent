# Creator Partner Program Guide

This is the rulebook for Westline creator partners. If you hold a current Creator Partner Agreement,
this document is your primary reference and it tells you which other Westline policies bind you.

Staff and contractors are not covered by this guide and cannot read it; questions about the programme
from inside Westline go to Creator Partnerships or to a People & Culture partner. See `HANDBOOK` §2.

## 1. What a creator partner is

### 1.1 The relationship

A creator partner is an independent creator — an influencer, a citizen journalist, a specialist
commentator — who publishes work under a Westline banner under a Creator Partner Agreement. You run
your own channel and your own business. Westline commissions work from you, distributes it, sells
sponsorship against it, and pays you per the rate card in §4.

You are not a Westline employee and not a Westline contractor. Those are three different
relationships with three different rulebooks, and the differences are not cosmetic.

### 1.2 What that means in practice

**You do not have.** Paid time off (see §5 for what replaces it), Westline group benefits other than
the assistance programme in `BENEFITS` §6, a performance review under `PERF`, expense reimbursement
under the staff sections of `EXPENSE`, statutory holiday pay, or a Westline-issued equipment kit.

**You do have.** A rate card and a payment term (§4), stated availability expectations rather than
scheduled hours (§5), ownership of everything you buy (§7), the right to work with other brands
subject to §6, access to the Employee & Family Assistance Program from day one (`BENEFITS` §6),
Field Safety desk support in the field (`SAFETY`), and the same protection against harassment as
anyone else at Westline (`CONDUCT`).

### 1.3 Tiers

| Tier | Typical profile | Base sponsored segment rate | Production allowance ceiling |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tier 1 | New to the programme, or fewer than 12 published segments | $850 | $350 |
| Tier 2 | Established; 12+ segments, sustained audience in a Westline market | $1,400 | $600 |
| Tier 3 | Anchor creator; exclusive category commitments, series commissions | $2,200 | $1,100 |

Tier is reviewed every six months by Creator Partnerships and is stated in your agreement. Tier
changes take effect on the first of the month following the review and are not applied retroactively
to invoices already submitted.

## 2. Which Westline policies apply to you

The authoritative matrix is `HANDBOOK` §2. Summarised for creator partners:

| Document | Applies? | What you need from it |
|---|---|---|
| `EDITORIAL` | In full | Standards and, critically, the disclosure rules in `EDITORIAL` §4. |
| `SAFETY` | In full | Field safety, crowds, weather, night work, and drones in `SAFETY` §5. |
| `INFOSEC` | In full | Accounts, devices, source material. |
| `SOCIAL` | In full | Your own channels as well as Westline's. |
| `CONDUCT` | §2, §4, §5, §7 | The standard of behaviour, public-facing conduct, raising a concern, escalation. |
| `EXPENSE` | §7 only | Invoicing. The rest of that document is for staff and contractors, and following it will give you the wrong answer. |
| `BENEFITS` | §6 only | The assistance programme. |
| `PTO`, `LEAVE`, `REMOTE`, `PERF` | Not at all | These are staff policies. |

## 3. Publishing obligations

### 3.1 Commissioning

Work is commissioned in writing by a Creator Partnerships producer, with a brief, a delivery date and
a rate reference. Nothing is owed for uncommissioned work, however good it is, and uncommissioned
work is not published under a Westline banner.

### 3.2 Editorial sign-off

Every sponsored segment is reviewed by the Editorial Standards desk before publication, with at least
**3 business days** for review, under `EDITORIAL` §4.5. Editorial sign-off covers standards and
disclosure. It is not a creative note and it is not optional.

### 3.3 Disclosure is not negotiable

Every sponsored segment carries the disclosure required by `EDITORIAL` §4 — the on-screen label, the
caption tag and, for audio, the spoken disclosure. The rules are the same for creator partners as for
staff. A sponsor asking for a "softer" disclosure is asking you to breach your agreement; refer them
to Creator Partnerships and do not negotiate it yourself.

### 3.4 Corrections

Corrections follow `EDITORIAL` §5 and `SOCIAL` §6: within 24 hours of becoming aware, on the same
channel and in the same format as the original.

## 4. Rate card and invoicing

### 4.1 What Westline pays for

Westline pays a **base segment rate** for the commissioned deliverable at your tier (§1.3), plus,
where the brief requires it, a **production allowance** up to your tier ceiling for costs incurred
making that specific segment: location fees, permits, a hired second operator, a rented lens, travel
that the brief requires, a licensed music cue.

The production allowance is agreed **in the commissioning brief, before the shoot**. It is not a
reimbursement scheme and it is not applied for afterwards. If the brief did not budget it, it is not
payable, and the answer is to raise it with your producer before you spend the money rather than
after.

### 4.2 What Westline does not pay for

Westline does not pay for equipment. Cameras, drones, lenses, lighting, audio kit, computers,
software subscriptions and phones are yours, they stay yours, and they are not reimbursable and not
claimable against a production allowance. §7 explains why, and `EXPENSE` §7.2 says the same thing
from the finance side.

Westline also does not pay for your ordinary cost of doing business: your own transport within your
home market, your own meals, your own insurance, your own accounting.

### 4.3 Invoicing

Invoice through the Creator Partnerships portal. Each invoice must reference the commissioning brief
number and separate the base segment rate from any agreed production allowance, with receipts
attached for the allowance line.

- Submit within **45 days** of the delivery date. Invoices submitted later than 45 days require
  Creator Partnerships approval and may be declined.
- Westline pays **Net 30** from the date a complete invoice is received. An invoice missing a brief
  number or missing receipts for an allowance line is not complete and the 30 days has not started.
- Payment is in Canadian dollars by direct deposit.

### 4.4 Taxes

You are responsible for your own income tax, GST/HST registration and remittance where applicable,
and any other charge arising from your business. Westline does not withhold, does not issue a T4, and
issues a T4A where the annual total requires it. Nothing in this programme makes you an employee for
tax purposes, and nothing in this guide is tax advice.

### 4.5 Usage and re-use

The base segment rate covers publication on Westline properties and Westline social channels, and
re-use in Westline promotional material, for **24 months**. Use of your segment by the sponsor on the
sponsor's own channels is a separate licence, negotiated per campaign, and is paid separately. If a
sponsor asks to "just repost it", that is a licence question — send them to Creator Partnerships.

## 5. Availability windows and time away

### 5.1 There is no paid time off

Creator partners do not accrue paid time off. `PTO` does not apply to you, you do not have a balance,
and there is nothing to request. What exists instead is the availability window.

### 5.2 Availability windows

Your agreement states an expected availability — typically a number of commissioned segments per
quarter and a responsiveness expectation for time-sensitive commissions. Availability is about
whether you can take a commission, not about hours worked or where you are.

### 5.3 Telling us you are unavailable

Give Creator Partnerships **10 business days' notice** of any period longer than **7 consecutive
days** in which you will be unavailable for commissions. Shorter absences need no notice; just answer
the brief or decline it.

Notice is not a request and is not approved or declined. It exists so that a producer does not build
a campaign around a creator who is away, and so that a quarter with a long absence in it is not
counted against your availability expectation at review.

### 5.4 Extended unavailability

Unavailability longer than **90 consecutive days** is reviewed by Creator Partnerships against your
agreement, and may result in a tier review or, by agreement, a pause on the agreement. Illness,
caring responsibilities and parental circumstances are not held against you; tell your programme
manager and it is handled as a pause rather than a performance matter.

## 6. Exclusivity and outside brand deals

### 6.1 Category exclusivity

Each sponsored campaign carries a **category exclusivity window of 30 days** from the publication
date of your final segment in that campaign. During that window you may not publish sponsored content
for a competing brand in the same product category, on any channel, including your own.

Tier 3 agreements may carry longer category commitments, stated in the agreement itself, and where
they do the agreement governs over this section.

### 6.2 Outside brand deals

You may work with brands outside Westline. That is the point of the programme; we commission you
because you have your own audience. Two obligations attach:

1. **Notice.** Give Creator Partnerships **10 days' written notice** before publishing sponsored work
   for a brand outside Westline. Notice names the brand and the product category so we can check it
   against any live exclusivity window under §6.1 and against any conflict under `EDITORIAL` §6.
2. **Disclosure.** Outside sponsored work is disclosed on your own channels to the same standard
   `EDITORIAL` §4 requires of Westline work. We hold you to our standard on your channel because your
   audience does not sort your posts by who commissioned them.

Notice is not approval-seeking. Westline can tell you a deal collides with a live exclusivity window;
it cannot tell you who else to work with.

### 6.3 Conflicts of interest

Where an outside relationship would make it improper for you to cover a subject — an investment, a
family interest, an ongoing paid relationship with an organisation you would be reporting on —
declare it to the Editorial Standards desk under `EDITORIAL` §6. Declaring a conflict is not an
admission of anything; failing to declare one is a breach of your agreement.

## 7. Equipment and ownership

### 7.1 Your equipment is yours

Everything you buy is yours. Westline does not fund, subsidise, co-own or reimburse creator partner
equipment purchases, and there is no approval route that changes that. If you buy a camera, a drone,
a gimbal, a microphone or a laptop to make Westline work, you own it, you insure it, you maintain it,
and you keep it if the agreement ends.

This is the single most common misunderstanding in the programme, and it usually arrives as "I bought
a drone for the shoot — can I expense it?" The answer is no. `EXPENSE` §7.2 states the same rule from
the finance side. What *is* payable is the production allowance in §4.1 for costs specific to a
commissioned segment where the brief budgeted them — and a **rental** of a drone or a lens for a
specific shoot can be an allowance line, where an outright **purchase** cannot.

### 7.2 The loaner programme

Creator Partnerships maintains a small pool of loaner equipment — cameras, drones, audio kits — in
Calgary and Vancouver, available for a commissioned shoot at no charge, booked at least 5 business
days ahead. If the brief needs kit you do not own, book a loaner rather than buying one. Loaners are
Westline property, are returned within 5 business days of the shoot, and damage beyond fair wear is
charged at repair cost.

### 7.3 Content ownership and licence

You own the copyright in your work. Westline takes the publication and re-use licence described in
§4.5. Raw footage stays yours except where a commissioning brief specifically buys it, which is rare
and always priced separately.

### 7.4 Drones specifically

A drone is equipment (§7.1) and its use is a safety matter (`SAFETY` §5). Both apply at once, and the
safety requirements do not soften because the drone is your own property: you need the appropriate
Transport Canada RPAS certificate, registration, and Field Safety desk approval **5 business days**
before the flight, exactly as staff do.

## 8. Ending the agreement

### 8.1 Notice

Either party may end a Creator Partner Agreement on **30 days' written notice**. Commissioned work in
flight is completed and paid, or cancelled and paid at 50% where Westline cancels it.

### 8.2 Immediate termination

Westline may end an agreement immediately for a breach of `EDITORIAL` (particularly an undisclosed
sponsorship), a breach of `CONDUCT`, a serious `SAFETY` breach, or a security incident caused by
disregard of `INFOSEC`.

### 8.3 What happens after

Published segments stay published under the licence in §4.5. Outstanding invoices are paid on their
normal terms. Loaner equipment is returned within 5 business days. Your own equipment is, as always,
your own.

## 9. Worked examples

### 9.1 A drone bought for a sponsored mountain shoot

A Tier 2 creator partner buys a drone to shoot a sponsored segment at a ski resort, then asks whether
they can expense it and whether the sponsor has to be disclosed. Four documents answer, and only one
of them is this one.

**The drone purchase is not reimbursable.** Equipment bought by a creator partner is the creator
partner's own property (§7.1), and `EXPENSE` §7.2 says the same from the finance side. There is no
approval route that changes it. What *would* have worked, before the money was spent: a **rental** for
the specific shoot is a production cost and can be an allowance line under `EXPENSE` §7.3, and the
loaner pool in §7.2 exists for exactly this — a drone booked five business days ahead at no charge.

**The production allowance is agreed before the shoot, not after.** Under §4.1 the allowance is set in
the commissioning brief. A Tier 2 ceiling is $600, but a ceiling is not an entitlement: if the brief
did not budget an allowance, nothing is payable, and the answer is to raise it with the producer
before spending rather than after.

**Disclosure is required and is measurable.** `EDITORIAL` §4.2 — an on-screen `SPONSORED BY [BRAND]`
label within the first five seconds, on screen at least three continuous seconds, at least 4% of frame
height. `EDITORIAL` §4.3 — `#Sponsored` or `#WestlinePartner` in the first two lines of the caption.
And under §3.2 the segment goes to the Editorial Standards desk at least three business days before
publication.

**Flying the drone has its own requirements.** `SAFETY` §5: the right Transport Canada RPAS
certificate, aircraft registration, an airspace check, $2,000,000 liability cover, and Field Safety
desk approval **five business days** before the flight. Owning the aircraft changes none of it
(§7.4). A ski resort is a crowd environment, so Advanced Operations and possibly a Special Flight
Operations Certificate may be needed — that is a `SAFETY` §5.2 question and it is not answered quickly.

### 9.2 An outside brand deal during a live exclusivity window

A creator partner finishes a sponsored campaign for an outdoor-apparel brand and is offered a deal by
a competing outdoor-apparel brand eighteen days later. §6.1 puts a 30-day category exclusivity window
on the first campaign from the publication date of the final segment, so publishing the second inside
that window would breach the agreement. The deal itself is fine; the timing is not, and waiting twelve
days resolves it.

The ten days' written notice in §6.2 is what surfaces this. Notice names the brand and the product
category precisely so Westline can check it against a live window and tell the creator partner before
they commit, rather than after.

A deal in an unrelated category — a coffee brand, say — is unaffected by the window and needs only the
notice and the disclosure.

### 9.3 Six weeks away

A creator partner is travelling for six weeks and will not take commissions. There is nothing to
request and no balance to draw on (§5.1). Under §5.3, give Creator Partnerships ten business days'
notice, because the period exceeds seven consecutive days. Notice is not approved or declined; it
exists so a producer does not build a campaign around someone who is away, and so the quarter is not
counted against the availability expectation at review.

Six weeks is well inside the ninety-day threshold in §5.4, so no agreement review is triggered.

### 9.4 A sponsor wanting to repost the segment

The base segment rate covers Westline properties, Westline social, and Westline promotional re-use for
24 months (§4.5). Use by the sponsor on the sponsor's own channels is a separate licence, negotiated
per campaign and paid separately. "Can we just repost it?" is a licence question, not a courtesy, and
it goes to Creator Partnerships.

### 9.5 An invoice paid late

An invoice submitted 30 days after delivery but missing the commissioning brief number is **not
complete**, so the Net 30 clock has not started (§4.3). Adding the brief number starts it. An invoice
submitted 60 days after delivery is past the 45-day window and needs Creator Partnerships approval,
which may be declined.

### 9.6 Asking about staff vacation entitlement

A creator partner asks how much vacation Westline gives. `PTO` is a staff document and is not
available to creator partners (`HANDBOOK` §2), so the honest answer is that the policy is not one this
programme can share, and that it would not answer the question anyway. The applicable answer is §5:
creator partners do not accrue paid time off, and the equivalent mechanism is the availability window.

## 10. Questions we are asked often

**Am I an employee?** No. §1.1. That is not a technicality — it is why `PTO`, `LEAVE`, `REMOTE` and
`PERF` do not apply to you and why you invoice rather than being paid a salary.

**Do I get benefits?** Only the Employee & Family Assistance Program, from day one, with no enrolment
step and no waiting period (`BENEFITS` §6). Westline never learns you used it.

**Can Westline tell me where to work, or when?** No. Your agreement sets availability expectations
(§5.2), not hours or location. `INFOSEC` and `SAFETY` still bind you wherever you work.

**Who signs off my segment?** The Editorial Standards desk, at least three business days before
publication, on the version that will actually go out (§3.2, `EDITORIAL` §4.5).

**Something went wrong in the field. Who do I tell?** The Field Safety desk, within four hours
(`SAFETY` §7.1). Trauma support afterwards comes through `BENEFITS` §6 and is arranged by the desk
regardless of class (`SAFETY` §7.4).

**Someone at Westline treated me badly. Do I have any recourse?** Yes. `CONDUCT` §2, §4, §5 and §7
apply to you. Raise it with Creator Partnerships or directly with a People & Culture partner; you do
not need to go through your producer, and retaliation for raising a concern is itself a breach
(`CONDUCT` §7.2).

**What happens to my published work if the agreement ends?** It stays published under the licence in
§4.5. Outstanding invoices are paid on normal terms. Loaners come back within five business days. Your
own equipment stays yours (§8.3).
